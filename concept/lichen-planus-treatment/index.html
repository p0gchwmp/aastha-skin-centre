Medical dermatology · Jammu

Lichen planus can affect more than skin.

This inflammatory immune-mediated condition may involve skin, mouth, lips, scalp, nails or genital areas. Site matters because symptoms, monitoring and treatment can differ substantially.

01 / What it is

An inflammatory disorder with several possible sites.

Lichen planus is believed to develop when immune activity targets cells in the skin or mucous membranes. A trigger is often not identifiable.

Skin

Itchy purple-brown or grey bumps.

Classic lesions may be flat-topped, grouped and marked by fine white surface lines.

Mouth

Lacy, red or erosive.

Oral disease can be asymptomatic or painful enough to affect eating and oral hygiene.

Scalp / nails / genital

Higher monitoring value.

These sites may persist longer and can cause scarring, nail damage or significant symptoms.

03 / Assessment

Site, morphology and medicine history matter.

Distribution
Skin, oral, scalp, nail or genital involvement is documented separately.
Appearance
Flat-topped lesions, white lacy lines, erosions, pigment change or scarring provide clues.
Medicines
Some medicines can cause a lichenoid drug eruption that resembles lichen planus.
Symptoms
Itch, burning, pain, ulcers, hair loss or nail damage influence urgency and treatment.
Biopsy / tests
Further investigation may be considered when the diagnosis is uncertain or the site needs closer evaluation.
04 / Treatment route

Control inflammation, protect function, monitor persistent sites.

01 / Confirm

Make sure the pattern fits.

Other rashes, oral conditions, drug eruptions and genital disorders can resemble lichen planus.

02 / Map

Document every affected site.

Treatment priorities differ between skin, mouth, scalp, nails and genital mucosa.

03 / Control

Reduce active inflammation.

Topical or other prescription treatments may be selected according to site, severity and disease extent.

04 / Protect

Prevent avoidable damage.

Scalp follicles, nails and painful mucosal areas may need earlier attention because damage can have longer-term effects.

05 / Review

Persistent oral or genital disease deserves follow-up.

Symptoms, healing and any suspicious change are reviewed rather than treating every flare as a one-off event.

05 / FAQ

Practical questions.

Is lichen planus contagious?

No. It is an inflammatory condition and does not spread through touching, towels, food, kissing or living together.

Is genital lichen planus an STI?

No. Genital lichen planus is not a sexually transmitted infection, although other genital conditions can look similar and diagnosis should be confirmed.

Can medicines cause a similar rash?

Yes. Some medicines can produce lichenoid eruptions. Do not stop prescribed medication independently; discuss the relationship with the treating doctors.

Can skin lichen planus leave dark marks?

Yes. Post-inflammatory pigmentation can remain after active lesions settle, particularly in medium-to-deep skin tones.
