Dark circles · Jammu

Darkness can be pigment, vessels or shadow.

Under-eye darkness can come from melanin, visible vessels, tear-trough hollowing, puffiness, inflammation or a mixture. The cause matters more than the label “dark circles.”

01 / Overview

One appearance, several mechanisms.

Periorbital darkness may look brown, blue, purple, grey or simply shadowed. Some patterns are primarily pigment; others are vascular or structural.

Pigment

Brown / grey colour.

Genetics, sun, rubbing, eczema or contact dermatitis can contribute to periorbital hyperpigmentation.

Vascular

Blue / purple tones.

Thin under-eye skin and superficial vessels can create colour that pigment treatments do not correct.

Structural

Tear-trough shadow.

Inherited anatomy or age-related volume changes can create a shadow that looks like pigmentation.

Mixed

More than one factor is common.

Pigment, hollowing, puffiness and vascular visibility can coexist.

03 / Assessment

Look at colour, contour and skin quality together.

Colour
Brown/grey suggests pigment; blue/purple can suggest visible vessels or thin skin.
Contour
Tear-trough hollowing and under-eye bags can create or exaggerate shadow.
Inflammation
Eczema, allergy and rubbing can cause persistent post-inflammatory change.
Age / anatomy
Inherited structure and age-related skin or volume change may shape the treatment plan.
04 / Treatment route

Treat the dominant cause first.

01 / Classify

Separate pigment, vessels and shadow.

A mixed pattern is common, so one treatment may not solve every component.

02 / Calm

Control rubbing, eczema or contact irritation.

Inflamed eyelid skin should settle before aggressive pigmentation procedures.

03 / Pigment

Use pigment-directed treatment only when pigment is present.

Topical care or selected procedures may be considered according to the pigment pattern and skin sensitivity.

04 / Structure

Hollows and bags need a different discussion.

Structural concerns may need filler or a surgical opinion in selected cases rather than depigmenting treatment.

05 / Maintain

Protect thin under-eye skin.

Sun protection, gentle skincare and control of recurrent irritation may help limit recurrence.

05 / FAQ

Common questions.

Are dark circles only from lack of sleep?
No. Sleep can influence appearance, but persistent dark circles often involve pigment, vessels, anatomy, skin thickness or inflammation.
Can rubbing make dark circles worse?
Yes. Repeated rubbing can worsen eczema and post-inflammatory pigmentation.
Can filler remove pigmentation?
No. Filler may address selected structural hollowing; it does not remove true melanin pigmentation.
