Hair transplant · Jammu

Plan the future hairline, not just today’s gap.

Hair transplantation redistributes a limited donor supply. Diagnosis, future hair-loss progression, donor density, scalp health and realistic coverage expectations should come before surgical planning.

01 / What it is

A transplant moves follicles. It does not create an unlimited new supply.

Follicular-unit grafts are moved from donor areas—commonly selected areas at the back and sides of the scalp—to thinning or bald recipient areas.

SourceDonor hair

A finite supply must be protected.

TargetRecipient area

Coverage depends on graft availability.

DesignAngle + direction

Placement should respect natural growth.

Long termNative hair

Existing hair may continue thinning.

02 / Suitability

Not every type of hair loss belongs in surgery.

The old Aastha page emphasizes diagnosis first. Pattern hair loss may be suitable in selected patients, while active alopecia areata, inflamed scalp disease, rapid shedding or scarring conditions can change or postpone planning.

Potentially suitable

A confirmed permanent pattern, adequate donor density, reasonably stable progression, a healthy scalp, realistic expectations and willingness to follow aftercare support candidacy.

03 / Donor planning

The donor area sets the ceiling.

Density, hair-shaft thickness, hairs per follicular unit, curl, scalp flexibility, existing scars, miniaturisation and previous harvesting all affect how much can be safely moved.

  • Donor density
  • Hair-shaft thickness
  • Hairs per follicular unit
  • Hair curl and colour
  • Scalp flexibility
  • Existing scars
  • Miniaturisation
  • Previous harvesting
  • Future surgical needs
04 / Technique

FUE and FUT are harvesting strategies—not quality labels.

Neither technique is automatically best for everyone. Their trade-offs involve scar pattern, graft harvesting, donor preservation, hairstyle preference and future planning.

FUE

Follicular units are removed individually with small circular punches. This avoids one long strip scar but can leave numerous small dot scars and can overharvest donor areas if poorly planned.

  • No long strip scar
  • Often suits shorter hairstyles
  • Individual graft harvesting
  • Still produces scars

FUT

A narrow strip of donor scalp is removed and divided into follicular-unit grafts. It generally leaves a linear scar that may be hidden by surrounding hair.

  • Can support larger graft harvests
  • May preserve donor distribution
  • Linear scar must be acceptable
  • Suitability depends on scalp assessment
05 / Marketing terms

Technique names should not replace medical planning.

The original page specifically notes that “DHI” is commonly used as a marketing term for graft placement with an implanter device. In many cases follicles are still harvested using an FUE-type method. Clinic-specific technique claims should only be advertised when equipment and operating-team details are verified.

Useful question to ask

What exactly changes in harvesting, graft handling, implantation, donor management and postoperative care—not just what the package is called?

06 / FAQ

Realistic surgical expectations.

Is FUE scarless?

No. FUE leaves multiple small punctate scars rather than one long linear scar. “Scarless” should not be promised.

Can a transplant stop future hair loss?

No. Transplanted follicles and native hair are different issues. Existing non-transplanted hair may continue to thin, so long-term planning matters.

Who is usually assessed for transplantation?

Selected patients with permanent patterned hair loss and adequate donor supply are commonly assessed, but scalp health, progression and expectations matter.

Is DHI automatically better?

No. DHI generally describes an implantation method or device workflow. It does not automatically determine overall surgical quality or suitability.
