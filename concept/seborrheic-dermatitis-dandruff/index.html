Scalp + facial inflammation · Jammu

Dandruff that keeps coming back.

Seborrheic dermatitis is a recurrent inflammatory condition affecting oil-rich areas such as the scalp, hairline, eyebrows, ears, beard area, central chest and selected skin folds.

01 / Pattern

Flakes are only one part of the picture.

Mild dandruff and more inflamed seborrheic dermatitis sit on a spectrum, but other scalp conditions can look similar.

Scalp dandruff

White or grey flakes with mild itching may represent the milder end of the seborrheic-dermatitis spectrum.

02 / Flares

A flare often has context.

  • Stress
  • Cold, dry weather
  • Heat and sweating
  • Irritating hair products
  • Fragranced skincare
  • Harsh scrubbing
  • Unsuitable cleansing frequency
  • Illness or changes in general health
03 / Differentiate

Dandruff, psoriasis and fungal infection are not interchangeable.

Scalp scale can come from different causes. Pain, broken hairs, smooth bald patches, thick plaques, pus or marked inflammation can point away from simple dandruff.

Seborrheic dermatitis

  • Often greasy or powdery scale
  • May involve eyebrows, ears or beard
  • Usually recurrent and non-contagious

Needs another pathway

  • Patchy hair loss or broken hairs
  • Very thick plaques or nail changes
  • Pus, pain, scarring or severe inflammation
04 / Control

Treat the flare. Then plan maintenance.

01Confirm pattern

Scalp, face, ears, folds and hair-loss clues are examined.

02Reduce scale + inflammation

Medicated cleansers, shampoos or anti-inflammatory treatment may be selected by site and severity.

03Adjust routine

Washing frequency and product selection are matched to scalp oiliness and sensitivity.

04Maintain

Because recurrence is common, intermittent maintenance may be discussed.
