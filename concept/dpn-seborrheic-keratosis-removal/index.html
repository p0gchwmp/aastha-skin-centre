Benign skin growths · Jammu

Remove the growth after naming it.

DPN and seborrheic keratoses are usually benign, but dark or raised lesions can resemble moles, warts and other growths. Diagnosis should come before cautery, freezing or cosmetic removal.

01 / What these growths are

Common and usually benign — but not interchangeable.

Dermatosis papulosa nigra usually appears as multiple small dark or skin-coloured raised growths, commonly on the face and neck in medium-to-deep skin tones. Seborrheic keratoses are common benign waxy, rough or “stuck-on” growths that become more common with age.

DPN

Small facial growths with a genetic tendency.

DPN may begin in adolescence or early adulthood and become more numerous with age.

Seborrheic keratosis

Waxy or rough growths.

These may be tan, brown, black, white or skin-coloured and are usually benign.

Key rule

Changing lesions get assessed.

Bleeding, rapid growth, several colours, irregular borders or an unusual appearance should not be dismissed as cosmetic.

03 / Assessment

Removal method follows lesion type, site and skin tone.

Appearance
Size, shape, colour, surface and whether lesions are all similar.
Symptoms
Bleeding, itch, pain, recurrent irritation or rapid growth can change priorities.
Skin tone
Post-inflammatory darkening or lightening risk matters particularly in melanin-rich skin.
Scar tendency
Personal keloid or abnormal-scar history is reviewed before elective removal.
Diagnosis
Dermoscopy or biopsy may be considered when the growth is atypical or uncertain.
04 / Treatment route

Do not start with the removal device.

01 / Identify

Confirm what the growth is.

DPN, seborrheic keratosis, wart, mole and other lesions can look similar to patients.

02 / Prioritise

Separate routine from suspicious.

Changing, bleeding or atypical lesions are assessed before elective cosmetic removal.

03 / Choose

Select a method that fits the lesion.

Electrosurgery, curettage, cryotherapy, shave removal or another approach may be considered depending on lesion type and location.

04 / Heal

Expect a healing phase.

Crusting, temporary pigment change and local tenderness can occur after removal.

05 / Review

Benign growths can recur or new ones can appear.

Removal clears treated lesions; it does not guarantee that genetically or age-related growths never return elsewhere.

05 / FAQ

Practical questions.

Is DPN a mole?

No. DPN can look like tiny moles but is generally a benign epidermal growth rather than a melanocytic naevus.

Is DPN contagious?

No. DPN is not contagious and is not caused by poor hygiene.

Does every seborrheic keratosis need removal?

No. Stable benign lesions may be left alone unless they are irritating, cosmetically concerning or diagnostically uncertain.

When should a growth be checked?

Arrange assessment if it changes rapidly, bleeds, develops several colours, ulcerates, has an irregular border or looks unlike the other growths.
