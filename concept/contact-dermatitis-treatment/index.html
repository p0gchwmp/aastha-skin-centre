Contact dermatitis · Jammu

Find the contact behind the rash.

Contact dermatitis is an inflammatory reaction caused by exposure to something in the environment. Irritant and allergic forms can look similar, so distribution, timing and exposure history matter.

Book rash assessmentExplore patterns ↓
01 / What it is

A rash can map the exposure.

Contact dermatitis may cause itching, burning, dryness, swelling, blisters, oozing, crusting, scaling or painful cracks. The rash often appears where the responsible substance contacts the skin.

Irritant

Barrier damage.

Soap, detergent, sanitiser, bleach, wet work, solvents and repeated water exposure can directly injure the skin barrier.

Allergic

Delayed immune reaction.

Nickel, fragrance, preservatives, hair dye, rubber additives, adhesives and acrylates are common examples.

Timing

Not always immediate.

Allergic reactions may appear hours or days after exposure, even to a product used safely for years.

Important

Not contagious.

Several people can react to the same chemical, but contact dermatitis itself does not spread from person to person.

03 / Assessment

The detective work matters.

Exposure
Products, work materials, gloves, metals, cosmetics, hair dye, skincare and recent changes.
Distribution
Hands, eyelids, neck, scalp, feet or another pattern can suggest different exposures.
Timing
Immediate irritation and delayed allergy have different histories.
Patch testing
May be considered when allergic contact dermatitis is suspected.
04 / Care route

Control the rash. Then control the exposure.

01 / Confirm

Separate dermatitis from its look-alikes.

Eczema, fungal infection, psoriasis and other rashes may overlap clinically.

02 / Calm

Repair the barrier and reduce inflammation.

Management can include moisturising, gentle cleansing and prescription anti-inflammatory treatment when appropriate.

03 / Identify

Look for repeatable exposures.

History and, in selected cases, patch testing can help identify a relevant allergen.

04 / Avoid

Substitution beats endless flare treatment.

Once a trigger is identified, reducing or replacing exposure becomes part of treatment.

05 / Maintain

Protect high-risk skin.

Barrier care, work practices and product choices may help reduce recurrent flares.

05 / FAQ

Common questions.

Can a familiar product suddenly cause allergy?
Yes. Allergic contact dermatitis can develop after repeated exposure even when a product was previously tolerated.
Is contact dermatitis contagious?
No. It is an inflammatory reaction, not an infection passed between people.
Is every hand rash an allergy?
No. Irritant damage from wet work, soap and chemicals is common, and mixed irritant-allergic patterns can occur.
