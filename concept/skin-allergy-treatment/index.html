Itching, rashes & allergy · Jammu

An itchy rash is a symptom, not a diagnosis.

Eczema, contact dermatitis, hives, infection and medicine reactions can overlap in appearance. The pattern, timing and exposures matter before treatment is chosen.

Selected pattern

Contact dermatitis

A rash can develop where an irritant or allergen repeatedly touches the skin. Hands, face and eyelids are common problem areas.

Products, work exposure, jewellery, hair dye and repeated wet work may matter.
02 / Assessment

The exposure history can be as important as the rash.

Distribution
Where the rash begins, where it spreads and which areas are spared can narrow the diagnosis.
Timeline
New products, work exposures, medicines, jewellery, hair dye, gloves and wet work may be relevant.
Look-alikes
Eczema, contact dermatitis, hives, fungal disease, scabies and medicine reactions may overlap.
Patch testing
May be considered when recurring delayed contact allergy is suspected, especially involving hands, face or eyelids.
03 / Care pathway

Control the flare. Then reduce the reason it keeps returning.

01 / Confirm

Make sure “allergy” is the right category.

Examination helps distinguish inflammatory dermatitis from infection, hives and other rash patterns.

02 / Trace

Look for what is touching or changing the skin.

Skincare, cosmetics, work exposure, gloves, detergents, medicines and hobbies may reveal a repeat trigger.

03 / Control

Settle active inflammation safely.

Treatment depends on the diagnosis, severity, body site, infection risk and medical context.

04 / Prevent

Barrier care and avoidance can reduce recurrence.

When a trigger is identified, practical avoidance and maintenance may be as important as medication.

!
Seek urgent hospital care for breathing difficulty, difficulty swallowing, tongue or throat swelling, faintness, or extensive blistering/skin peeling.

Do not wait for a routine clinic appointment or WhatsApp reply in these situations.
