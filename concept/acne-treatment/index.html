01 / What acne is

More than “just pimples.”

Acne is an inflammatory condition involving blocked hair follicles, oil production and inflammation. It may affect the face, chest, shoulders and back, and can leave red marks, dark marks or permanent textural scars.

Why treatment matters

Control active breakouts. Reduce scar risk.

Treatment aims to control existing acne, prevent new lesions, reduce the risk of permanent scars and establish an effective maintenance routine.

A
Age

Teenage or adult.

Acne commonly affects teenagers and young adults, but it may continue into adulthood or begin for the first time later in life.

Not always acne

Look-alikes exist.

Folliculitis, perioral dermatitis and other acne-like eruptions can resemble acne but need different treatment.

Impact

Skin is only part of the story.

Acne may affect confidence and emotional wellbeing as well as the skin. Severity and psychological effect both matter when planning care.

04 / Why acne happens

It is not simply “dirty skin.”

Acne develops through a combination of blocked follicles, excess oil, inflammation and changes in organisms normally present on the skin. Hormonal activity and genetic tendency can also influence it.

Internal factors

Hormones & genetics.

Hormonal changes and family tendency can influence when acne appears and how it behaves.

Products & friction

What touches the skin matters.

Oily or pore-blocking products, helmets, masks and tight clothing may worsen breakouts in some people.

Treatment habits

Picking and stopping early.

Picking lesions, unsuitable skincare and stopping treatment too early can worsen inflammation or reduce long-term control.

Important myth

Acne is not caused by poor hygiene alone.

Aggressive scrubbing can irritate the skin and may make inflammation worse.

05 / When to see a dermatologist

Some acne deserves earlier assessment.

Consider a dermatology consultation when acne is painful or deep, is leaving scars or persistent dark marks, affects the chest or back extensively, does not improve with basic care, keeps returning, begins suddenly in adulthood, causes emotional distress, appears different from typical acne, or is associated with other hormonal symptoms.

  • Painful deep lesions or rapid scarring
  • Persistent post-acne marks
  • Repeated recurrence despite treatment
  • Extensive face, chest or back involvement
  • Sudden adult-onset acne
  • Possible hormonal symptoms such as irregular periods or unwanted hair growth
  • Significant impact on confidence or daily life

07 / Treatment route

A treatment plan should have an order.

There is no single acne treatment suitable for everyone. Acne type, severity, body area, age, pregnancy considerations, previous response and scar tendency all matter.

01 / Confirm

First make sure it really is acne.

Acne-like eruptions can resemble acne but need different treatment, so examination matters before escalation.

02 / Assess

Severity and scar risk change the plan.

Lesion type, distribution, duration, pain, marks, scars and previous treatment response help determine treatment intensity.

03 / Control

Active breakouts usually come first.

Management may involve topical treatment, oral medicines, skincare adjustments or selected in-clinic procedures depending on individual assessment.

04 / Residual concerns

Marks and scars are different problems.

Post-acne pigmentation and true textural scars need different strategies. Scar-specific procedures are generally planned after active acne is controlled.

05 / Maintain

Acne can recur.

Maintenance, trigger management and follow-up may be part of the plan when appropriate.

09 / Acne FAQ

Questions patients usually ask.

Is acne caused by poor hygiene?

No. Acne is not simply caused by poor hygiene, and aggressive scrubbing may irritate the skin and worsen inflammation.

Can adult acne start later in life?

Yes. Acne may persist into adulthood or begin later, including patterns around the lower face, jawline and chin.

Do all patients need blood tests?

No. Tests are not required for every patient. Blood tests or hormonal assessment may be considered when the history or examination suggests an underlying issue.

Are acne marks the same as acne scars?

No. Red or dark marks are colour changes after inflammation, while acne scars are permanent textural changes.

When should scar treatment start?

Scar-specific planning is generally considered after active acne is adequately controlled, because ongoing breakouts can continue to create new scars.

Persistent or scarring acne deserves proper assessment.
