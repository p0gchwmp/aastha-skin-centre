Raised scars · Jammu

Not every raised scar behaves the same way.

Hypertrophic scars stay within the original wound. Keloids can grow beyond it and may recur after treatment. Scar type, symptoms, location and previous response shape the plan.

02 / Assessment

The scar history changes the treatment strategy.

Trigger
Surgery, piercing, acne, burn or another injury can help explain the scar pattern.
Growth
Whether the scar stayed within the wound or extended beyond it helps distinguish hypertrophic scar from keloid.
Symptoms
Itching, tenderness, pain, tightness and movement restriction are assessed.
Previous treatment
Response to injections, silicone, laser or surgery matters when planning the next step.
03 / Treatment route

Flattening a raised scar is usually a sequence, not a one-step promise.

01 / Classify

Define the scar before selecting the procedure.

Boundary, thickness, age, location, symptoms and growth pattern help distinguish scar types.

02 / Prioritise

Reduce pain, itch or restriction where present.

The treatment goal may be symptom relief, flattening, colour improvement or a combination.

03 / Treat

Use the least aggressive effective combination.

Depending on assessment, options may include silicone therapy, pressure, intralesional medicine, selected laser treatment or surgery/referral.

04 / Recurrence

Plan for the scar’s tendency to return.

Selected keloids may need recurrence-prevention treatment rather than excision alone.

✦
A previous keloid increases risk after future piercing, tattoos or elective skin procedures.

If you form keloids easily, discuss that history before creating a new skin injury.
