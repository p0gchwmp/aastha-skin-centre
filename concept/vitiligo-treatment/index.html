Depigmentation · Jammu

White patches need a diagnosis first.

Vitiligo is an autoimmune pigment condition in which melanocytes are lost from affected areas. Not every white patch is vitiligo, and the pattern, activity and distribution matter before treatment.

01 / Pattern

Distribution tells part of the story.

Non-segmental vitiligo

The most common type often affects both sides of the body and may involve hands, face and body openings.

02 / Differentiate

Not every pale patch is vitiligo.

Tinea versicolor, pityriasis alba, post-inflammatory hypopigmentation, chemical leukoderma, previous burns and other disorders can create pale or white skin.

Vitiligo

  • Usually smooth white depigmented patches
  • Often sharply defined
  • Not contagious
  • Can involve hair within the patch

Another cause may fit when

  • There is scale or active inflammation
  • The colour loss follows a rash, burn or chemical exposure
  • The pattern is atypical or stable from birth
03 / Activity

Stable and actively spreading vitiligo are different clinical situations.

01Map existing patches

Distribution, borders, hair colour and mucosal involvement are documented.

02Ask about change

New patches, enlarging areas or colour loss after injury can indicate activity.

03Confirm diagnosis

Clinical examination and, when helpful, a Wood’s lamp can support assessment.

04Plan treatment + review

Options depend on site, activity, age, extent and previous response.

✦
Vitiligo is not caused by poor hygiene or “wrong food combinations.”

It is an autoimmune pigment disorder. Myths around cleanliness or diet can create unnecessary stigma and should not replace medical assessment.
