Melasma treatment · Jammu

Control the trigger, not just the colour.

Melasma is a chronic, relapsing facial pigmentation condition. Treatment commonly combines photoprotection, gentle skincare, prescription treatment and maintenance; procedures may be added selectively.

01 / Recognise the pattern

Melasma is not every dark patch.

It commonly causes flat brown or grey-brown patches on the cheeks, forehead, upper lip, nose, chin or jawline. Freckles, post-acne marks and deeper pigment disorders can look similar but behave differently.

02 / Triggers

Why does it keep returning?

The older Aastha guide identifies sunlight, visible light, hormonal influences, heat, irritation and individual susceptibility among important contributors.

  • Sun exposure
  • Visible light
  • Pregnancy
  • Hormonal medicines
  • Genetic tendency
  • Heat exposure
  • Irritating skincare
  • Certain cosmetics
  • Stopping maintenance too early
03 / Long-term control

Melasma is usually managed in layers.

One aggressive peel or laser is not a universal answer. A dermatologist may build treatment from protection upward and add procedures only when appropriate.

Control hierarchy

Protect daily

Broad-spectrum sunscreen, visible-light protection where appropriate, shade and reapplication form the base of relapse prevention.

01

Photoprotection

Sunlight and visible light can worsen melasma. Tinted sunscreen with iron oxides may be considered where appropriate.

02

Skin barrier

Scrubbing and irritating products do not remove melasma and can make pigmentation harder to control.

03

Topical treatment

Strength, combination and duration depend on skin sensitivity, diagnosis and pregnancy status.

04

Procedures

Procedures are adjuncts, not substitutes for diagnosis and photoprotection.

05

Stay controlled

Stopping all treatment immediately after improvement can allow triggers to re-darken pigmentation.

04 / Consultation

What gets assessed?

  • Pattern and colour
  • Likely pigment depth
  • When it began
  • Pregnancy or hormonal medicines
  • Previous creams, peels or lasers
  • Acne, eczema or irritation
  • Sun, visible-light and heat exposure
  • Current skincare and cosmetics
  • Signs of another pigment disorder
05 / FAQ

The questions that change expectations.

Can melasma be permanently cured?

Melasma commonly behaves as a chronic, relapsing condition. Treatment can improve control, but recurrence can occur when triggers remain active.

Can pregnancy cause melasma?

Yes. Hormonal changes during pregnancy can trigger or worsen melasma in susceptible people, and some medicines are unsuitable during pregnancy.

Why is sunscreen part of treatment?

Ultraviolet and visible light can worsen pigmentation. Photoprotection helps treatment work and reduces relapse pressure.

Does everyone need laser?

No. Melasma care commonly starts with protection, skincare and medical treatment. Laser or peel procedures are considered selectively.
