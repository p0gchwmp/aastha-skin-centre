Rosacea care · Jammu

Redness has a pattern.

Rosacea is a long-term inflammatory condition that can cause flushing, persistent facial colour, visible vessels, acne-like bumps, burning, sensitivity and eye symptoms.

01 / Overview

More than facial redness.

Rosacea commonly affects the cheeks, nose, forehead and chin, but may also involve the eyes, eyelids, ears, scalp, neck or upper chest.

Early pattern

Flushing, heat, sensitivity.

Episodes of facial warmth, colour change, burning or stinging may come before persistent signs.

Inflammation

Bumps without blackheads.

Papules and pustules can resemble acne, but typical blackheads and whiteheads are not the defining pattern.

Vessels

Visible superficial vessels.

Persistent colour and telangiectasia may become more noticeable over time.

Eyes

Ocular symptoms matter.

Dry, gritty, burning, bloodshot or light-sensitive eyes can be part of rosacea and sometimes need eye assessment.

02 / Pattern explorer

Rosacea does not look the same on everyone.

Selected pattern

Flushing / persistent colour

Warmth, flushing and persistent red, brown, dusky or violet facial colour may dominate the presentation.

In deeper skin tones, burning and sensitivity can be more obvious than bright redness.

03 / Triggers

Triggers provoke flares. They do not define the disease.

Heat
Heat, hot showers and temperature changes can provoke flushing in some patients.
04 / Assessment

Treat the dominant signs.

Colour
Flushing, persistent colour and visible vessels may need a different emphasis from inflammatory bumps.
Skin sensitivity
Burning, stinging and product intolerance influence skincare and topical treatment.
Eyes
Ocular symptoms can change the treatment plan and may require ophthalmology input.
Look-alikes
Acne, eczema, contact dermatitis, steroid reactions and other conditions can resemble rosacea.
05 / Care route

Control the flare. Protect the barrier. Treat the sign.

01 / Confirm

Separate rosacea from acne and dermatitis.

Dominant signs, distribution, triggers and eye symptoms help establish the likely pattern.

02 / Barrier

Gentle skincare is treatment, not decoration.

Cleansing, moisturising and sun protection can reduce irritation and support prescription treatment.

03 / Medical

Topical or oral treatment may target inflammation.

The choice depends on the dominant features and severity.

04 / Light-based care

Visible vessels need a different tool.

Selected vascular redness may be considered for IPL or another suitable light/laser pathway.

05 / Maintain

Rosacea can recur.

Long-term trigger awareness, barrier care and maintenance treatment may be needed.

06 / FAQ

Common questions.

Is rosacea contagious?
No. Rosacea is an inflammatory condition and is not spread by touch, towels, cosmetics or food.
Is rosacea the same as acne?
No. They can both cause facial bumps, but blackheads and whiteheads are not typical rosacea features.
Can rosacea affect the eyes?
Yes. Dryness, burning, gritty sensation, eyelid inflammation and visual symptoms can occur.
