01 / Scar morphology

Texture is not one diagnosis.

Dark or red post-acne marks can exist without a permanent textural scar. True scars may be narrow and deep, broad and shallow, tethered or raised.

Selected scar pattern

Rolling scars

Broad, shallow depressions can create a wavy surface. Some are tethered by deeper fibrous bands, which changes procedural planning.

02 / Before treatment

Active acne usually comes first.

Continuing breakouts can create new scars while older scars are being treated. Scar work is commonly planned after acne is reasonably controlled.

03 / Treatment planning

Different tools solve different structural problems.

Possible options in the original Aastha pathway include MNRF, microneedling, subcision, fractional CO₂ laser, focal techniques, peels for associated pigmentation and selected combination plans.

Treatment logic

Release tethering

When fibrous bands pull selected scars downward, subcision may be considered to release deeper tethering.

01

Subcision

Best thought of as a structural tool for selected tethered scars rather than a universal acne-scar procedure.

02

MNRF

May be considered for rolling, selected boxcar and mixed atrophic scars. Several sessions may be recommended according to severity and response.

Marks ≠ scars

A peel or pigment strategy cannot physically lift a deep depressed scar, while resurfacing alone may not address every colour change.

05 / Practical questions

Before you commit to a scar plan.

Can one session remove acne scars?

Acne scars vary in type and severity. Improvement is usually staged, and multiple sessions or combined methods may be recommended. No responsible plan should promise complete removal in one session.

Are acne marks the same as acne scars?

No. Flat red, brown, grey-brown or purple marks may be post-inflammatory colour changes without a permanent change in skin texture.

Why might more than one procedure be recommended?

Many patients have mixed scar patterns. A technique that helps tethering may not address a narrow deep scar, while resurfacing may not solve all structural problems.

When should scar treatment begin?

It commonly begins after active acne is reasonably controlled, although the dermatologist may continue acne treatment or maintenance during the scar-treatment course.
