Patchy hair loss · Jammu

A smooth bald patch is a pattern, not a verdict.

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune, usually non-scarring hair-loss condition. It may cause one smooth patch or much more extensive loss, and the course can be unpredictable.

01 / Extent

Alopecia areata can behave in very different ways.

Patchy alopecia areata

One or several smooth round or oval patches on the scalp, beard or body are the most familiar presentation.

02 / Clinical clues

The skin usually looks surprisingly normal.

Smooth skin, preserved follicular openings and specific hair or nail clues can support the diagnosis. Scale, pus, pain or scarring may point elsewhere.

  • Smooth round or oval bald patches
  • Short broken or exclamation-mark hairs
  • Fine regrowing hairs
  • White or grey initial regrowth
  • Patchy beard loss
  • Eyebrow or eyelash loss
  • Nail pitting or roughness
  • Tingling, itching or burning in some patients
03 / Differentiate

Patchy hair loss has look-alikes.

Typical alopecia areata

  • Smooth non-scarring patch
  • Usually little scale or crust
  • Follicular openings preserved

Needs another diagnosis considered

  • Marked scale, pus or broken hairs
  • Shiny scarred skin or loss of follicular openings
  • Significant pain or inflamed plaques
04 / Assessment

Extent, activity and associated clues shape the plan.

  • Number, size and location of patches
  • How quickly new areas are appearing
  • Scalp, beard, eyebrow or eyelash involvement
  • Nail changes
  • Family or personal autoimmune history
  • Signs suggesting fungal or scarring hair loss
  • Previous episodes and treatments
  • Emotional and practical impact

Regrowth is possible, but not predictable.

Because follicles are generally not permanently destroyed, regrowth may occur. The amount, timing and durability differ substantially between patients and disease extent.
