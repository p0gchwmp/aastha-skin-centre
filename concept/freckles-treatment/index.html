Freckles & sun spots · Jammu

Not every brown spot is a freckle.

True freckles, solar lentigines, melasma, post-inflammatory pigmentation and moles can overlap visually. Diagnosis comes before cosmetic treatment.

01 / What they are

Small spots can tell different stories.

True freckles, or ephelides, are usually small, flat and influenced by genetics and ultraviolet exposure. Solar lentigines reflect accumulated sun exposure and tend to persist throughout the year.

Freckles

Often childhood-onset.

They darken with sun exposure and can become lighter during winter.

Sun spots

Usually persist.

Solar lentigines are more common in adulthood and reflect cumulative ultraviolet exposure.

Indian skin

Pigment risk matters.

Inflammation from procedures can leave darker or lighter marks, so settings and technique need caution.

Safety

Changing spots deserve assessment.

A new, irregular, bleeding or evolving pigmented lesion should not be treated cosmetically before examination.

04 / Treatment route

Protect first. Treat selectively.

01 / Confirm

Make sure the spot is suitable for cosmetic treatment.

Freckles, lentigines, melasma and pigmented lesions should be distinguished first.

02 / Protect

UV control reduces new pigment stimulation.

Sun protection remains relevant even if a procedure is chosen.

03 / Medical

Some pigmentation responds to topical care.

Topical treatment may be discussed depending on the diagnosis and pigment pattern.

04 / Procedure

Selected spots may suit peel or pigment-targeting treatment.

Q-switched laser, chemical peels or another approach may be considered according to diagnosis and skin tone.

05 / Maintain

Sun-related pigment can recur.

Future exposure can produce new freckles or lentigines even after existing spots fade.

05 / FAQ

Common questions.

Are freckles a disease?
No. Ordinary freckles are a benign pigment characteristic.
Are freckles and sun spots the same?
No. Freckles often begin in childhood and may fade seasonally; solar lentigines are usually persistent adult sun-related spots.
Can new spots appear after treatment?
Yes. UV exposure and individual tendency can lead to new freckles or lentigines over time.
