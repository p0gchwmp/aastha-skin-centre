Medical dermatology · Jammu

Hives that appear, move, disappear.

Urticaria causes transient raised wheals, itching and sometimes angioedema. Acute episodes, chronic spontaneous urticaria and physically triggered hives follow different diagnostic pathways.

01 / Type

First ask: how long, how often, and what reliably triggers it?

Acute urticaria

Episodes present for less than six weeks. Infection, medicines, food allergy, stings or other immune triggers may be considered from the history.

02 / Physical triggers

Some hives are reproducible. Others are not.

DermatographismRaised itchy lines after scratching, rubbing or pressure.PressureDelayed swelling after belts, straps, tight footwear or sustained pressure.ColdWheals or swelling after cold air, water, ice or chilled objects.Heat / sweatingCholinergic urticaria may follow exercise, hot showers or body-temperature rise.SunlightSolar urticaria is triggered by light exposure in selected patients.ContactSome hives follow direct contact with a triggering substance.
03 / Appearance

On deeper skin tones, elevation may be easier to feel than redness is to see.

Typical hives

  • Raised bumps or larger swollen patches
  • Rings, irregular shapes or lines after scratching
  • Intense itching, burning or stinging
  • Individual lesions flatten without permanent marks

Indian skin tones

  • May be skin-coloured, purple, grey or dark brown
  • Swelling can be more obvious than redness
  • Side lighting can make wheals easier to see
  • Photos during a flare can help when lesions disappear before consultation
✦
Chronic spontaneous urticaria is usually not a simple food allergy.

True food allergy tends to have a reproducible timing relationship to a specific food and may include mouth or throat itching, vomiting, breathing difficulty, dizziness or anaphylaxis. Chronic hives often continue without one repeatable food trigger.

04 / Assessment

A useful history beats a random panel of tests.

  • When the hives started
  • How long each individual lesion lasts
  • How often new wheals appear
  • Whether angioedema occurs
  • Medicines and recent infections
  • Consistent food or sting relationships
  • Pressure, cold, heat, exercise or sunlight triggers
  • Photographs of episodes that disappear before the visit
  • Symptoms suggesting a different diagnosis
  • Impact on sleep, work or daily activity
