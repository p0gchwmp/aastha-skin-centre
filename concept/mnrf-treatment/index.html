01 / How it works

Needles create access. RF creates controlled heat.

The original guide distinguishes MNRF from ordinary microneedling and from non-invasive RF tightening. The procedure creates mechanical and thermal treatment zones at planned depths.

01Needles enter

Fine electrodes penetrate to selected depths according to the area and scar pattern.

02RF energy is delivered

Radiofrequency produces small controlled thermal zones within and beneath the skin.

03Healing follows

The controlled injury triggers a healing and remodelling response over time.

04Texture is reviewed

Several sessions may be needed and response is reassessed rather than assuming one fixed protocol.

02 / Scar matching

One device does not fit every scar.

Rolling, boxcar and ice-pick scars can need different combinations. Raised scars and keloids follow a different pathway.

Rolling scars

MNRF may support dermal remodelling, but tethered rolling scars may also need subcision.

03 / Assessment

The consultation decides whether MNRF belongs in the sequence.

  • Confirm whether active acne is controlled
  • Classify rolling, boxcar and ice-pick scars
  • Check for tethering beneath rolling scars
  • Assess scar depth and distribution
  • Review pigmentation tendency and skin tone
  • Review previous peels, lasers or scar procedures
  • Ask about keloid or abnormal-scar history
  • Review medicines and medical conditions

Safety detail

A new sterile needle cartridge should be used for every patient and every treatment session. Reusing a cartridge is unsafe and can spread infection.

04 / Compare

MNRF vs ordinary microneedling.

Microneedling

Creates controlled punctures using fine needles without radiofrequency energy.

  • Mechanical needling
  • No RF heating
  • Different treatment intensity and risk profile

MNRF

Combines needle penetration with controlled radiofrequency heating at selected depths.

  • Mechanical + thermal zones
  • Depth and energy are adjustable
  • Medical procedure, not a home dermaroller treatment
✦
Stronger is not automatically better.

Depth, RF energy, pulse duration, needle type and passes all affect result and risk. Scar-specific planning matters more than simply increasing settings.
