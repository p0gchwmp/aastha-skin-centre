03 / Causes

The cause list is broad for a reason.

Pattern hair loss

Male and female androgenetic patterns can progress gradually and may benefit from earlier treatment aimed at preserving existing follicles.

Telogen shedding

Illness, surgery, childbirth, stress and other physiological changes can trigger diffuse shedding.

Autoimmune

Alopecia areata can cause smooth patches and may involve nails or more extensive hair loss.

Nutritional & hormonal

Iron deficiency, thyroid or hormonal conditions and nutritional factors may contribute in selected patients.

Scalp disease

Dandruff, psoriasis, infection and inflammatory scalp disease can contribute to shedding or discomfort.

Hair practices

Tight hairstyles, heat, chemical damage and traction can injure the hair or follicles.

05 / When to come in

Some hair-loss patterns should not be watched indefinitely.

What signs deserve earlier assessment?
Sudden heavy shedding, bald patches, scalp pain or burning, significant redness or scaling, pustules, eyebrow or eyelash loss, rapidly changing density, or signs of scarring are among the reasons the original page recommends dermatology assessment.
Do supplements work for everyone?
No. The original guide emphasizes identifying the cause and correcting a confirmed deficiency rather than automatically starting supplements for every form of hair loss.
Does every patient need PRP or GFC?
No. Procedures are supportive options in selected cases and should follow diagnosis rather than replace it.
