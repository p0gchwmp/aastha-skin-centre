Photodamage · Jammu

Treat the visible damage. Protect what remains.

Repeated ultraviolet exposure can affect pigment, texture, vessels, collagen and skin-cell DNA. Some visible signs can improve, but accumulated cellular sun damage cannot simply be erased.

01 / What sun damage is

UV injury accumulates quietly.

Photoageing is premature visible skin ageing caused mainly by ultraviolet exposure. Pigment changes, roughness, fine lines, reduced elasticity and visible superficial vessels can emerge years after the exposure responsible for them.

Pigment

Sun spots and uneven tone.

Solar lentigines, freckles and blotchy pigmentation may reflect cumulative exposure.

Texture

Roughness and fine lines.

UV exposure affects collagen, elastin and surface texture over time.

Safety

Not every “sun spot” is cosmetic.

A changing, bleeding, irregular or non-healing lesion should be medically assessed before treatment.

03 / Assessment

Separate pigment, texture, vessels and suspicious lesions.

Lesion type
Freckles, lentigines, melasma, PIH and raised growths are not treated as one category.
Skin tone
Melanin-rich skin may have a greater tendency toward post-inflammatory darkening after aggressive treatment.
Sun exposure
Current exposure, tanning and sunscreen habits affect both treatment timing and recurrence.
Texture / laxity
Surface roughness, fine lines and loss of firmness may require different tools from isolated pigment.
Suspicious change
Biopsy or treatment of a precancerous/cancerous lesion takes priority over cosmetic improvement when indicated.
04 / Treatment route

Protect first. Then treat the correct target.

01 / Protect

Stop adding new UV injury.

Broad-spectrum sun protection, shade and practical exposure reduction support every other treatment.

02 / Diagnose

Decide which spots are safe to treat cosmetically.

Changing or suspicious lesions are assessed separately before peels, IPL or pigment laser treatment.

03 / Pigment

Match the pigment to the tool.

Prescription skincare, chemical peels, Q-switched laser or IPL may be considered for selected pigment patterns.

04 / Texture

Texture needs a different target.

MNRF or fractional resurfacing may be considered for selected texture or photoageing concerns after skin type and recovery are assessed.

05 / Maintain

Recurrence is part of the conversation.

Ongoing photoprotection and suitable skincare help protect gains and reduce new visible damage.

06 / FAQ

Practical questions.

Can sun-damaged skin be completely repaired?

Some visible signs can improve, but accumulated cellular ultraviolet damage cannot be completely erased.

Is a tan a sign of sun damage?

Yes. Tanning develops after ultraviolet exposure stimulates pigment production following skin-cell injury.

Can lasers or peels remove sun spots?

Selected sun spots may improve with peels, Q-switched laser or IPL after diagnosis and skin type are assessed. Recurrence and pigment change remain possible.

When should a sun spot be checked?

Seek assessment if a spot changes, bleeds, develops irregular borders or several colours, becomes rough or fails to heal.
