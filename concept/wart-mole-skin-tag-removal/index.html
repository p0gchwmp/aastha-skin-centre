01 / Identify

“Small bump” is not a diagnosis.

A suspected skin tag may be a wart, mole, seborrhoeic keratosis or another lesion. The clinical appearance decides what should happen next.

Wart

Warts are usually non-cancerous HPV-related growths. They may be rough, flat, thread-like, clustered or thickened on the sole.

02 / When to check

Changing, bleeding or unusual deserves assessment first.

  • Rapid change in size or shape
  • New or uneven colour
  • Bleeding or persistent crusting
  • New pain or significant itching
  • A lesion that looks unlike the person’s other moles
  • A growth that repeatedly returns after treatment
  • Open sore or ulcer-like change
  • A new rapidly growing lesion in adulthood
03 / Mole warning guide

ABCDE is a prompt to look closer — not a self-diagnosis.

AAsymmetry

One half differs from the other.

BBorder

An irregular, blurred or poorly defined edge can warrant assessment.

CColour

Several colours or uneven pigmentation may be clinically relevant.

DDiameter

Size can matter, although suspicious lesions can also be smaller than 6 mm.

EEvolving

Change in size, shape, colour or symptoms is particularly important.

04 / Removal options

The technique depends on what is being removed.

Possible wart pathways

  • Salicylic-acid treatment
  • Cryotherapy
  • Electrosurgery / curettage
  • Selected prescription or procedural options

Possible mole / benign growth pathways

  • Shave removal for suitable raised lesions
  • Excision when depth or histology matters
  • Technique selected around site and scar risk
  • Laboratory examination when clinically indicated
✦
Do not burn, cut or acid-treat an uncertain mole at home.

Home destruction can injure the skin and can remove or alter a lesion before it has been properly assessed.
