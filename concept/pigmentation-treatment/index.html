Melasma

Brown or grey-brown facial patches are often influenced by sunlight, visible light, hormones and individual susceptibility. Melasma is commonly chronic and relapsing.

02 / Assessment

Before treatment, establish the depth and the cause.

Pattern & distribution

Colour, location, symmetry and distribution can help distinguish different pigmentary disorders.

Triggers & history

Sun and heat exposure, pregnancy or hormones, acne, rashes, products, medicines and previous procedures may matter.

Investigations when useful

Dermatoscopy, Wood’s lamp or selected blood tests may be considered when they can change the assessment. Not every patient needs tests.

03 / Treatment logic

Different depth. Different tool.

The original page is explicit that one brightening cream, peel or laser is not appropriate for every pigmentation concern.

Treatment layer

Protect first

Daily photoprotection and gentle skincare are part of treatment and relapse prevention, especially for melasma and post-inflammatory pigmentation.

01

Photoprotection & trigger control

Broad-spectrum sunscreen, shade and where appropriate tinted sunscreen can be part of the plan. Active acne, eczema or irritation should also be controlled.

02

Topical medical treatment

Depending on the diagnosis, options may include hydroquinone-based preparations, retinoids, azelaic acid, cysteamine, kojic acid or other medical depigmenting agents.

03

Peels, lasers & procedures

Aggressive or unsuitable treatment can worsen pigmentation, especially in darker skin tones, so procedure selection matters.

04

Prevent recurrence

Long-term management matters because improvement does not always mean the tendency to pigment has disappeared.

04 / Common variants

Pigmentation shows up in different ways.

Post-acne marks

Flat brown, grey, red or purple marks after acne are colour changes rather than true textural scars. Ongoing acne can create new marks.

Dark neck

Friction, inflammation or acanthosis nigricans can contribute. In some cases the assessment may extend beyond cosmetic treatment.

Dark lips

Genetics, irritation, products, smoking, sunlight, inflammation or medical causes can influence lip pigmentation.

Dark circles

Pigment, visible vessels, thin skin, allergy, shadowing and tear-trough anatomy can overlap.

Deeper pigment

Some acquired dermal pigment disorders can resemble melasma but behave differently.

Sun damage

Freckles and lentigines are sun-related but should still be correctly identified before cosmetic treatment.

05 / FAQs

The practical questions.

Can one laser treat every type of pigmentation?
No. Pigment depth, diagnosis, skin tone and previous response all affect whether a laser is suitable. Some pigmentary conditions are managed primarily with medical treatment and photoprotection.
Why can pigmentation come back?
Conditions such as melasma are often chronic and influenced by sunlight, visible light, hormones and individual susceptibility. Maintenance may be required.
Is sunscreen part of treatment?
Yes. The original guide treats photoprotection as part of the treatment and relapse-prevention plan, not as an optional extra.
