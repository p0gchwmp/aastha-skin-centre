Injectables · Jammu

Treat the anatomy, not the trend.

Botulinum toxin and dermal fillers are different medical procedures. One temporarily reduces selected muscle activity; the other adds or restores volume in carefully assessed areas.

01 / Two different tools

Movement and volume are not the same problem.

The original page separates botulinum toxin from fillers because their mechanisms, goals, duration and risk profiles differ.

Botulinum toxin

Temporarily reduces activity in selected muscles. Cosmetic use may soften movement-related lines while preserving natural expression when appropriately planned.

  • Frown lines
  • Forehead expression lines
  • Crow’s-feet
  • Selected lower-face muscle concerns

Dermal fillers

Add or restore volume in selected facial areas. Product, tissue plane, anatomy and treatment goal all matter.

  • Cheek volume
  • Chin balance
  • Lip definition
  • Selected folds or contours
02 / Assessment

The face should be assessed at rest and in motion.

A good injectable consultation is not a preset injection map. Facial movement, symmetry, proportions, previous treatment, skin condition, medical history and the desired amount of change all influence planning.

  • Face at rest
  • Face during movement
  • Symmetry and proportions
  • Previous toxin or filler

  • Medicines and history
  • Desired amount of change
  • Risks and alternatives
03 / Natural expression

“Frozen” should not be the goal by default.

Botulinum toxin is used to reduce selected muscle activity. The treatment area, dose and injection points depend on anatomy, muscle strength and how much movement the patient wants to retain.

05 / FAQ

Questions worth asking before injectables.

Are botulinum toxin and fillers the same?

No. Botulinum toxin reduces selected muscle activity. Fillers add or restore volume. Some patients may be suitable for one, both or neither.

Does botulinum toxin add volume?

No. It does not replace facial volume; it acts on selected muscles.

Should every person get the same injection map?

No. Facial anatomy, muscle strength, asymmetry, medical history and desired movement vary between people.

Can fillers copy a filtered photograph?

That should not be the planning goal. Suitability should follow real facial anatomy, proportions, safety and realistic expectations.
