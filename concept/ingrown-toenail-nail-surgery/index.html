Ingrown toenail & nail surgery · Jammu

A painful nail edge can become a recurring problem.

Ingrown toenails range from mild tenderness to swelling, discharge, overgrown tissue and repeated infection. Treatment depends on severity, recurrence and medical risk.

01 / Severity explorer

The treatment changes as the nail becomes more inflamed or recurrent.

Selected stage

Mild ingrown nail

Tenderness and slight redness may occur without pus or significant tissue overgrowth.

Early cases may improve with pressure reduction and appropriate conservative care.
02 / Assessment

Look at the nail, the pressure and the reason it keeps happening.

Nail edge
One or both sides may be embedded into the adjoining skin.
Infection
Pus, spreading redness, warmth and significant swelling change treatment priorities.
Contributors
Cutting nails too short, rounded corners, tight footwear, trauma and naturally curved nails can contribute.
Medical context
Diabetes, circulation, immunity, medicines and healing history may affect procedure planning.
03 / Care pathway

Relieve the pressure now. Reduce recurrence where possible.

01 / Grade

Assess severity, infection and recurrence.

The nail edge, surrounding tissue, discharge, granulation tissue and previous episodes are reviewed.

02 / Conservative care

Mild cases may improve without surgery.

Pressure reduction, wide footwear, warm soaks and allowing the nail to grow can be part of early management when appropriate.

03 / Procedure

Recurrent or severe cases may need nail surgery.

Depending on the problem, part of the nail may be removed under local anaesthetic; the exact procedure is selected after assessment.

04 / Prevention

Reduce repeated pressure and avoid digging into the corners.

Trimming toenails straight across and wearing shoes that do not compress the toes can help reduce recurrence.

✦
Avoid home nail removal, deep V-shaped cutting, unsterile blades and repeatedly digging into the corners.

These can injure the nail fold and worsen infection or recurrence.
