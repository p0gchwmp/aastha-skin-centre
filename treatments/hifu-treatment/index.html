What is HIFU?

HIFU stands for high-intensity focused ultrasound.

Aesthetic focused-ultrasound devices deliver energy beneath the skin to create controlled heating in selected tissue layers. This controlled response may support tissue contraction and gradual collagen remodelling. The FDA classifies focused-ultrasound systems for aesthetic use as devices intended to produce temporary changes in physical appearance through localised tissue effects.

The results vary between devices, treatment protocols and patients. Evidence from one HIFU or microfocused-ultrasound platform should not automatically be applied to every machine.

What concerns may HIFU address?

After clinical assessment, HIFU may be considered for selected concerns such as:

Mild lower-face laxity

Early jowling

Reduced jawline definition

Loose skin beneath the chin

Mild neck laxity

Selected brow or facial-lifting concerns

Gradual age-related loss of firmness

Systematic reviews suggest that focused or microfocused ultrasound can produce measurable improvement in suitable patients with mild-to-moderate facial laxity, although the visible change is generally modest and study protocols vary.

Who may be suitable for HIFU?

HIFU may be considered for an adult who:

Has mild or moderate skin looseness

Wants a non-surgical treatment

Understands that improvement develops gradually

Does not expect a surgical facelift result

Has no active infection or open wound in the treatment area

Is medically suitable for the specific device

Can follow treatment and aftercare instructions

Patients with severe laxity, substantial excess skin or major facial-volume loss may receive limited improvement from HIFU alone. The 2023 systematic review found that results tended to be less favourable when laxity was more advanced.

Which areas may be treated?

Depending on the patient’s anatomy and the indications of the particular device, HIFU may be considered for:

Lower face

Jawline

Area beneath the chin

Neck

Selected cheek areas

Selected brow or forehead concerns

Treatment near the eyes, major nerves, glands, bone or important blood vessels requires careful anatomical planning.

The clinic should not publish a fixed list of device-approved areas until the machine’s model, instructions and supporting records have been documented.

How does HIFU work?

The treatment applicator directs ultrasound energy to planned depths beneath the skin. Energy is concentrated within small target zones, creating controlled heating while aiming to minimise unnecessary exposure to surrounding tissue.

The tissue response may involve:

Immediate contraction within treated tissue

A healing response

Gradual collagen remodelling

Progressive improvement in firmness

The FDA identifies thermal injury, mechanical injury, ocular injury and use error among the risks that manufacturers and operators must address for focused-ultrasound systems.

Is HIFU painful?

HIFU is not completely painless for every patient.

Possible sensations include:

Tingling

Heat

Brief pinprick sensations

Deep aching

Tenderness over bony areas

Short electrical-like sensations

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis found moderate average procedural discomfort, while common short-term reactions included redness, swelling, bruising and tenderness.

Comfort varies according to treatment location, energy settings, anatomy and individual sensitivity.

Is anaesthesia required?

HIFU is usually performed without general anaesthesia.

Depending on the device, treatment area and patient sensitivity, the clinic may discuss:

No anaesthesia

Topical numbing treatment

Oral pain relief where medically suitable

Cooling or treatment modifications

Patients should not take pain-relieving or anti-inflammatory medicines before treatment unless advised, because their medical history and existing medicines must first be considered.

Is there downtime after HIFU?

Many patients return to routine activities after treatment.

Temporary effects may include:

Redness

Mild swelling

Tenderness

Tingling

Bruising

Temporary numbness

Sensitivity when touching the treated area

These effects are generally mild and temporary in published studies, although uncommon serious complications have been reported.

When will HIFU results become visible?

HIFU results are not usually immediate or dramatic.

The body requires time to remodel collagen. Improvement may become more visible over approximately two to six months, although the exact timeline varies.

Possible changes may include:

Mildly improved firmness

Softer appearance of early jowling

Improved lower-face definition

Reduced appearance of mild submental laxity

Gradual improvement in selected lines

The final result should be assessed with consistent photographs rather than day-to-day mirror checks.

How many HIFU sessions are required?

Some patients undergo one initial treatment followed by clinical review. Others may be advised to have an additional treatment depending on:

Device protocol

Degree of laxity

Treatment area

Age

Skin thickness

Previous procedures

Response to the first session

Maintenance goals

AAD guidance notes that many people receive one ultrasound treatment, while some may gain additional benefit from further sessions.

A fixed package should not be prescribed before examination.

How long do HIFU results last?

The duration varies because:

Natural ageing continues

Collagen response differs

Sun exposure affects the skin

Smoking affects tissue quality

Weight changes alter facial contours

The treatment device and settings differ

The starting degree of laxity differs

Long-term evidence remains limited, and studies use different devices and follow-up periods. One systematic review noted that robust longer-term data beyond approximately one year were lacking in the included research.

Maintenance treatment may be discussed after the response has been reviewed.

Can HIFU replace a facelift?

No.

HIFU cannot:

Remove substantial excess skin

Reposition tissue as extensively as surgery

Produce a full surgical neck lift

Correct severe jowling

Permanently stop ageing

Guarantee a sharp jawline

Non-invasive skin-tightening procedures provide considerably less lifting than facelift, neck-lift or eyelid surgery.

A surgical opinion may be more appropriate when the patient wants major structural correction.

What are the risks of HIFU?

Temporary reactions may include:

Pain

Redness

Swelling

Bruising

Tenderness

Tingling

Numbness

Rare or more serious complications reported with microfocused-ultrasound treatment or device surveillance include:

Burns

Skin injury

Persistent altered sensation

Nerve-related symptoms

Scarring

Localised tissue or fat loss

Asymmetry

Unsatisfactory results

A 2025 systematic review found that serious events were uncommon in published studies but identified reports of lipoatrophy, neurological symptoms and scarring in device-reporting data.

Who may need to avoid or postpone HIFU?

Treatment may require postponement or additional review when there is:

Pregnancy or breastfeeding

Open wounds

Active skin infection

Severe inflammatory or cystic acne in the area

Active herpes

A pacemaker or implanted defibrillator

Metallic implants within the treatment area

Reduced sensation

Recent facial surgery

Recent filler or thread treatment

An uncontrolled medical condition

Unrealistic expectations

FDA guidance identifies open wounds, severe or cystic acne, active implanted devices and metallic implants among important contraindication considerations for focused-ultrasound devices.

The specific machine’s instructions must always take priority.

Can HIFU be performed after fillers or threads?

Previous fillers or threads must be disclosed.

The doctor may review:

Product used

Area treated

Treatment date

Injection depth

Previous complications

Thread location

Desired result

Treatment may need to be delayed, modified or performed in a different anatomical plane. HIFU should not be applied blindly over recently treated tissue.

Is HIFU suitable for Indian skin?

Focused ultrasound does not target melanin in the same way as pigment-specific lasers, so it may be considered across a range of skin tones.

However, it is not risk-free. Burns or inflammation can still produce:

Dark marks

Lighter patches

Prolonged redness

Scarring

Sensitivity

Skin tone, skin condition, treatment settings and aftercare remain important.

What aftercare may be recommended?

Patients may be advised to:

Use a gentle cleanser

Apply a simple moisturiser

Use sunscreen

Avoid excessive rubbing

Avoid strong exfoliating products temporarily

Avoid another energy-based procedure until advised

Report severe pain, blistering or persistent weakness

Attend the recommended review

The exact instructions depend on the treatment area, device and skin response.

Frequently asked questions

Is HIFU effective for loose skin?

HIFU may provide modest tightening in suitable patients with mild-to-moderate facial or neck laxity. It cannot provide the same correction as surgery.

How long does HIFU take to show results?

Improvement commonly develops gradually over approximately two to six months as tissue remodelling occurs.

Is HIFU painful?

Patients may experience tingling, warmth, pinprick sensations or deep aching. Published reviews describe average discomfort as mild-to-moderate or moderate.

How many HIFU sessions are needed?

Some patients receive one initial session, while others may benefit from additional treatment. The plan depends on the device, treatment area and individual response.

Can HIFU remove a double chin?

HIFU skin-tightening treatment may improve selected laxity beneath the chin, but it should not automatically be presented as a fat-removal treatment. A double chin may involve fat, skin laxity or both.

Can HIFU cause facial fat loss?

Unwanted localised tissue or fat loss has been reported, although significant complications appear uncommon in published studies. Careful patient selection, anatomical planning and device settings are important.
