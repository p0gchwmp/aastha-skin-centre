What is a chemical peel?

A chemical peel is a controlled skin-resurfacing procedure in which a selected solution is applied to the skin for a planned duration.

The solution causes controlled exfoliation of particular skin layers. As the treated skin heals, it may appear smoother, more even and less congested.

Chemical peels are also called:

Chemexfoliation

Medical peels

Skin-resurfacing peels

Dermatological peels

A chemical peel should not be confused with an ordinary salon facial. The treatment involves medically active solutions that can cause burns, pigmentation changes or scarring when they are used incorrectly. The FDA has warned against purchasing or using strong chemical-peel products without appropriate professional supervision.

What types of chemical peels are there?

Chemical peels are generally classified according to how deeply they affect the skin.

Superficial peels

Superficial peels act mainly on the outer skin layers.

They may be considered for:

Mild acne

Blackheads

Oily skin

Post-acne marks

Mild pigmentation

Dullness

Superficial roughness

They usually involve less recovery than deeper peels, although temporary redness, dryness or flaking can still occur.

Medium-depth peels

Medium-depth peels reach further into the skin and involve greater recovery and complication risk.

They may be considered for selected pigmentation, texture or photoageing concerns after careful assessment.

Deep peels

Deep peels require substantially greater medical supervision and recovery. They are not a routine substitute for superficial dermatology peels and may require a surgical setting and anaesthesia.

The website should not imply that every peel depth is routinely performed at Aastha Skin Centre. The appropriate peel is decided after consultation.

Which peel ingredients may be used?

Different peel agents have different properties. Depending on the diagnosis and skin type, dermatologists may use formulations containing ingredients such as:

Salicylic acid

Glycolic acid

Lactic acid

Mandelic acid

Jessner-type combinations

Trichloroacetic acid in selected strengths

Retinoid-based peel formulations

Combination or proprietary medical peels

The ingredient name alone does not determine whether a peel is appropriate. Concentration, pH, number of layers, exposure time, skin preparation and neutralisation can all affect the treatment’s strength.

Patients should not compare two peels only by percentage. A higher percentage is not automatically safer or more effective.

A peel should form part of a complete acne plan rather than being sold as an instant cure.

Is a chemical peel suitable for Indian skin?

Chemical peels can be performed on Indian and other medium-to-deep skin tones, but peel selection and aftercare require particular care.

Deeper skin tones may have a greater tendency to develop:

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation

Uneven darkening

Temporary lightening

Prolonged irritation

Visible demarcation between treated and untreated skin

The American Academy of Dermatology advises patients with skin of colour to seek a dermatologist experienced in performing peels on similar skin tones because incorrect treatment can cause lasting pigment changes.

Treatment may begin conservatively and be adjusted according to response.

What happens after a chemical peel?

After a superficial peel, patients may notice:

Redness

Tightness

Dryness

Mild swelling

Flaking

Temporary sensitivity

Darkening before peeling

Increased sensitivity to sunlight

Healing time varies according to peel depth. The AAD notes that superficial peel recovery may take approximately one to seven days, while deeper treatment requires longer healing and more extensive wound care.

Not every superficial peel causes visible sheets of peeling. A peel can still have an effect without dramatic shedding.

Why should patients avoid home chemical peels?

Strong acids sold online may be incorrectly labelled, contaminated or far more concentrated than ordinary skincare products.

At-home chemical peels can cause:

Severe chemical burns

Painful wounds

Permanent pigmentation changes

Infection

Scarring

Eye injury

Long-term sensitivity

The FDA specifically warns consumers against using certain high-strength chemical-peel products without professional supervision because serious injuries have occurred.

Do not apply professional-strength glycolic acid, salicylic acid, lactic acid, TCA or phenol peels at home.

Can chemical peels make skin thin?

Appropriately selected and supervised peels do not simply make healthy skin progressively thinner.

Chemical peels create controlled exfoliation and healing. However, frequent, overly strong or incorrectly timed peels can damage the skin barrier and cause prolonged irritation.

Treatment intervals must allow the skin to recover.

Patients should not combine clinic peels with several strong home exfoliants unless advised.

Can chemical peels cause purging?

Some patients may notice temporary acne activity after changes in exfoliation or skincare.

However, new bumps may also represent:

Irritation

Folliculitis

Contact dermatitis

Acne flare

Occlusion from aftercare products

Infection

Severe, painful or persistent breakouts should be reviewed rather than automatically labelled as normal “purging.”
