What is cryolipolysis?

Cryolipolysis is a non-invasive procedure that applies controlled cold to a selected layer of fat beneath the skin.

During treatment, a suitable fold of pinchable fat may be drawn into an applicator using vacuum. The tissue is cooled for a device-specific period, which may be up to approximately one hour. Fat cells are more sensitive to controlled cooling than the surrounding skin, so the treatment is intended to damage selected fat cells without making an incision.

The damaged fat cells are gradually cleared by the body over the following months, producing a reduction in the visible fat bulge rather than immediate weight loss.

How does Dr. Cheena Langer assess body-contouring suitability?

A consultation may include:

  • Identifying the patient’s main concern
  • Assessing whether the fullness is fat, loose skin or both
  • Checking whether the fat is pinchable
  • Reviewing weight stability
  • Examining skin quality and sensation
  • Looking for scars, hernias or muscle separation
  • Reviewing previous surgery or liposuction
  • Checking for cold-sensitivity conditions
  • Reviewing medicines and medical history
  • Discussing the likely degree of visible change

The treatment area and applicator should be chosen according to anatomy—not simply according to the patient’s preferred package.

What happens during cryolipolysis?

A typical treatment appointment may involve:

  • Confirming and photographing the area
  • Marking the treatment boundaries
  • Checking the skin for wounds or irritation
  • Applying a protective barrier where required
  • Positioning the applicator
  • Drawing suitable tissue into the applicator
  • Delivering controlled cooling
  • Monitoring skin contact and patient comfort
  • Removing the applicator
  • Performing device-specific post-treatment care

The treated area may look firm, raised or moulded to the applicator immediately after treatment before gradually returning towards its usual shape.

Types, options and detailed guidance

Is cryolipolysis the same as fat freezing?

Yes. Fat freezing is the common patient-facing term for cryolipolysis.

However, not every machine marketed as fat freezing has the same:

  • Cooling control
  • Applicator design
  • Safety system
  • Approved treatment areas
  • Clinical evidence
  • Regulatory status

Device details and regulatory claims depend on the exact system used and are discussed during consultation.

What does body contouring mean?

Body contouring refers to treatments intended to change the shape or silhouette of a selected body area.

Depending on the technology, body-contouring procedures may aim to:

  • Reduce a small localised fat bulge
  • Reduce the circumference of a selected area
  • Improve the appearance of cellulite
  • Improve selected muscle tone
  • Support mild skin firmness

Cryolipolysis specifically targets selected subcutaneous fat. It does not cut away skin or physically suction fat from the body.

Is cryolipolysis a weight-loss treatment?

No.

Cryolipolysis:

  • Does not treat obesity
  • Does not provide meaningful overall weight loss
  • Does not replace diet and physical activity
  • Does not improve metabolic health
  • Does not treat diabetes, cholesterol or fatty liver
  • Does not provide the health benefits associated with medical weight reduction

The FDA states that non-invasive body contouring reduces small localised fat bulges rather than producing weight loss.

A 2025 systematic review found improvement in several local body measurements after cryolipolysis but did not find a statistically significant reduction in overall body weight.

Which type of fat can cryolipolysis treat?

Cryolipolysis is intended for subcutaneous fat—the soft, pinchable fat located beneath the skin.

It is not designed to directly treat visceral fat, which surrounds internal organs inside the abdomen.

A suitable area generally needs:

  • A defined localised bulge
  • Enough soft tissue for the applicator
  • Healthy overlying skin
  • No significant circulation problem
  • No underlying hernia or weak abdominal-wall area
  • Realistic expectations

A hard or rounded abdomen caused mainly by visceral fat is unlikely to respond like a superficial pinchable fat fold.

Which areas may be considered?

Depending on the specific machine, applicator and documented indications, cryolipolysis may be considered for selected areas such as:

  • Abdomen
  • Flanks or love handles
  • Back or bra-line bulges
  • Upper arms
  • Inner or outer thighs
  • Area beneath the chin
  • Other suitable localised fat pockets

Not every applicator is authorised or anatomically suitable for every body area.

A final list of treatment areas should only be published after the clinic records the cryolipolysis machine’s model, applicators and supported indications. The verified clinic checklist specifically advises against unsupported device claims.

Who may be suitable for cryolipolysis?

Treatment may be considered for an adult who:

  • Has a stable or relatively stable weight
  • Has a localised pinchable fat bulge
  • Does not need treatment for obesity
  • Understands that improvement will be limited
  • Prefers a non-surgical procedure
  • Has healthy skin over the treatment area
  • Has no cold-sensitivity disorder
  • Has no hernia or major structural weakness in the area
  • Can wait several months for the result
  • Accepts the possible risks and need for further treatment

Cryolipolysis works best as a contouring procedure for a selected area—not as a way to reduce overall body size.

Who may not get enough improvement?

Cryolipolysis may provide limited benefit when there is:

  • Generalised obesity
  • Predominantly visceral abdominal fat
  • Considerable loose skin
  • A large amount of excess fat
  • Significant abdominal-muscle separation
  • A need for substantial weight reduction
  • An expectation of liposuction-like change
  • An expectation of immediate results
  • A desire for skin removal

Patients with substantial excess skin may need a surgical opinion rather than a fat-freezing procedure.

What does cryolipolysis feel like?

At the beginning of treatment, patients may feel:

  • Strong pulling or suction
  • Intense cold
  • Pressure
  • Tingling
  • Pinching
  • Aching
  • Temporary discomfort

The area may become numb as cooling continues.

After treatment, patients may experience:

  • Tenderness
  • Tingling
  • Cramping
  • Sensitivity
  • Temporary numbness
  • Mild pain

The procedure should not be advertised as completely painless.

Is anaesthesia required?

Cryolipolysis normally does not require:

  • General anaesthesia
  • Surgical incisions
  • Stitches
  • Hospital admission

Pain relief is not routinely required for every patient, but comfort varies.

Patients should inform the clinic if they develop severe, sharp or unexpected pain during treatment so the applicator and skin can be checked.

Is there downtime after cryolipolysis?

Many patients return to ordinary activities after treatment.

Temporary effects may include:

  • Redness
  • Swelling
  • Bruising
  • Tenderness
  • Firmness
  • Tingling
  • Cramping
  • Altered sensation
  • Numbness
  • Skin discolouration

The FDA notes that persistent numbness, nodules and cold-related burns may also occur, although these are less common.

When do cryolipolysis results become visible?

Results develop gradually rather than immediately.

The body’s immune system clears damaged fat cells over approximately two to three months, although visible change may continue beyond that period.

Patients may notice:

  • Gradual reduction in the treated bulge
  • Improved clothing fit
  • A smoother body outline
  • A modest change in local circumference

A 2025 systematic review found reductions in abdominal circumference and local fat thickness at approximately 12 weeks, although results varied considerably between studies.

How much fat reduction can be expected?

There is no single percentage that applies to every patient, treatment area or machine.

Outcome depends on:

  • Device and applicator
  • Cooling protocol
  • Area treated
  • Thickness of the fat layer
  • Number of treatment cycles
  • Patient anatomy
  • Weight changes
  • Individual biological response

Older systematic reviews reported measurable local reductions, but the included studies were often small and used different protocols and measurement methods.

A fixed percentage reduction cannot be guaranteed without device-specific evidence.

How many sessions are required?

Some patients may notice sufficient improvement after one treatment cycle. Others may require:

  • More than one cycle on the same area
  • Treatment of an adjacent area
  • A second appointment after results are reviewed
  • Another type of body-contouring procedure

No further treatment if the initial response is limited

The number of sessions depends on:

  • Size of the bulge
  • Applicator coverage
  • Symmetry
  • Desired contour
  • Response to the first treatment
  • Safe spacing between sessions

A fixed package should not be sold before anatomical assessment.

Additional care and practical advice

Cryolipolysis

  • Does not involve an incision
  • Uses controlled cooling
  • Treats selected pinchable fat
  • Produces gradual change
  • Usually involves less recovery
  • Provides limited local reduction
  • Cannot remove large amounts of fat

Liposuction

  • Is a surgical procedure
  • Uses a cannula to suction fat
  • May remove a larger volume
  • Requires anaesthesia
  • Has surgical risks
  • Involves a recovery period

The FDA distinguishes non-invasive body contouring from liposuction, which physically removes fat through a small incision.
