What is cryolipolysis?

Cryolipolysis is a non-invasive procedure that applies controlled cold to a selected layer of fat beneath the skin.

During treatment, a suitable fold of pinchable fat may be drawn into an applicator using vacuum. The tissue is cooled for a device-specific period, which may be up to approximately one hour. Fat cells are more sensitive to controlled cooling than the surrounding skin, so the treatment is intended to damage selected fat cells without making an incision.

The damaged fat cells are gradually cleared by the body over the following months, producing a reduction in the visible fat bulge rather than immediate weight loss.

Is cryolipolysis the same as fat freezing?

Yes. Fat freezing is the common patient-facing term for cryolipolysis.

However, not every machine marketed as fat freezing has the same:

Cooling control

Applicator design

Safety system

Approved treatment areas

Clinical evidence

Regulatory status

The website should use the generic term cryolipolysis unless the exact brand and model used by the clinic have been documented.

What does body contouring mean?

Body contouring refers to treatments intended to change the shape or silhouette of a selected body area.

Depending on the technology, body-contouring procedures may aim to:

Reduce a small localised fat bulge

Reduce the circumference of a selected area

Improve the appearance of cellulite

Improve selected muscle tone

Support mild skin firmness

Cryolipolysis specifically targets selected subcutaneous fat. It does not cut away skin or physically suction fat from the body.

Is cryolipolysis a weight-loss treatment?

No.

Cryolipolysis:

Does not treat obesity

Does not provide meaningful overall weight loss

Does not replace diet and physical activity

Does not improve metabolic health

Does not treat diabetes, cholesterol or fatty liver

Does not provide the health benefits associated with medical weight reduction

The FDA states that non-invasive body contouring reduces small localised fat bulges rather than producing weight loss.

A 2025 systematic review found improvement in several local body measurements after cryolipolysis but did not find a statistically significant reduction in overall body weight.

Which type of fat can cryolipolysis treat?

Cryolipolysis is intended for subcutaneous fat—the soft, pinchable fat located beneath the skin.

It is not designed to directly treat visceral fat, which surrounds internal organs inside the abdomen.

A suitable area generally needs:

A defined localised bulge

Enough soft tissue for the applicator

Healthy overlying skin

No significant circulation problem

No underlying hernia or weak abdominal-wall area

Realistic expectations

A hard or rounded abdomen caused mainly by visceral fat is unlikely to respond like a superficial pinchable fat fold.

Who may not get enough improvement?

Cryolipolysis may provide limited benefit when there is:

Generalised obesity

Predominantly visceral abdominal fat

Considerable loose skin

A large amount of excess fat

Significant abdominal-muscle separation

A need for substantial weight reduction

An expectation of liposuction-like change

An expectation of immediate results

A desire for skin removal

Patients with substantial excess skin may need a surgical opinion rather than a fat-freezing procedure.

When do cryolipolysis results become visible?

Results develop gradually rather than immediately.

The body’s immune system clears damaged fat cells over approximately two to three months, although visible change may continue beyond that period.

Patients may notice:

Gradual reduction in the treated bulge

Improved clothing fit

A smoother body outline

A modest change in local circumference

A 2025 systematic review found reductions in abdominal circumference and local fat thickness at approximately 12 weeks, although results varied considerably between studies.

Are the results permanent?

The damaged fat cells are intended to be cleared from the treated area. However, the procedure should not be described as producing guaranteed permanent body shape.

Remaining fat cells can enlarge with weight gain, and future changes may occur because of:

Ageing

Pregnancy

Hormonal changes

Weight fluctuation

Lifestyle

Changes in skin elasticity

Maintaining a stable weight can help preserve the visible contour.

Can cryolipolysis tighten loose skin?

Cryolipolysis is primarily intended to reduce selected fat bulges.

It does not:

Surgically remove excess skin

Reliably correct severe laxity

Replace an abdominoplasty

Guarantee skin tightening

Correct stretched abdominal muscles

A patient with both fat and loose skin may require a separate assessment for RF, HIFU, another skin-tightening treatment or surgical consultation.

Can cryolipolysis remove cellulite?

Cryolipolysis is not automatically a cellulite treatment.

Cellulite is influenced by:

Fibrous bands beneath the skin

Skin thickness

Fat distribution

Connective tissue

Hormones

Genetics

Reducing a local fat bulge may change the outline of an area without fully correcting dimpling or cellulite.

The treatment goal should be defined before the procedure.

What are the common side effects?

Common temporary effects include:

Redness

Swelling

Bruising

Pain or discomfort

Tenderness

Temporary skin discolouration

A systematic review and meta-analysis published in 2025 found that temporary numbness, redness, swelling, pain and altered sensitivity were among the most frequently reported adverse effects.

These reactions usually settle, but duration differs between patients.

What less common complications can occur?

Less common complications include:

When used under the jawline, reported risks also include temporary changes in lip or tongue position caused by nerve effects and a temporary sensation of fullness in the throat.

What is paradoxical adipose hyperplasia?

Paradoxical adipose hyperplasia, or PAH, is a rare but serious complication unique to cryolipolysis.

Instead of becoming smaller, the treated fatty tissue gradually becomes larger, firmer and more prominent. The enlargement commonly follows the shape of the applicator and may appear approximately two to five months after treatment.

PAH:

Does not usually disappear on its own

May become more visible over time

Can cause significant cosmetic distress

May require liposuction or surgical removal

May not be fully corrected by surgery

Recent medical literature suggests that PAH may be underreported and that its exact cause and optimal management remain uncertain.

Patients should be counselled about PAH before consenting to treatment.

Can cryolipolysis cause a hernia?

There have been reports of hernias developing or becoming apparent after treatment.

Cryolipolysis should not be performed:

Directly over a known hernia

Near a structurally weak abdominal-wall area

Over certain surgical scars without assessment

Over significant separation of abdominal muscles

Where tissue support is uncertain

The FDA specifically advises avoiding treatment in or near a pre-existing hernia or structurally weak areas such as surgical scars or abdominal separation following pregnancy.

Who should not undergo cryolipolysis?

Cryolipolysis should not be performed in patients with cold-sensitivity disorders such as:

Raynaud’s disease

Pernio or chilblains

Cold urticaria

Cryoglobulinaemia

Paroxysmal cold haemoglobinuria

Cold agglutinin disease

Poor circulation in the treatment area

These conditions can cause abnormal or dangerous reactions to cold exposure.

Is cryolipolysis suitable after pregnancy?

Treatment should only be considered after:

Pregnancy has ended

Breastfeeding considerations have been discussed

Weight has stabilised

Abdominal-wall separation has been assessed

Hernia has been excluded

Skin laxity has been distinguished from fat

Caesarean or other scars have been examined

Post-pregnancy abdominal fullness may result from fat, loose skin, muscle separation, hernia or a combination. Cryolipolysis addresses only suitable subcutaneous fat.

Is cryolipolysis suitable for Indian skin tones?

Cryolipolysis does not primarily target skin pigment, so it may be considered across a range of skin tones.

However, patients with medium-to-deep skin may still develop:

Post-inflammatory darkening

Lighter areas

Prolonged discolouration

Marks after cold injury

Visible scarring if a burn occurs

The FDA notes that darkening or lightening of the skin can occur after body-contouring procedures, including in people with darker skin types.
