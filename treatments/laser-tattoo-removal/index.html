What is laser tattoo removal?

Laser tattoo removal uses short pulses of concentrated light to target tattoo pigment beneath the skin.

When an appropriate laser wavelength reaches the ink:

The pigment absorbs the laser energy

The ink breaks into smaller particles

The surrounding immune system gradually clears some of those particles

The tattoo becomes lighter over successive sessions

The laser does not physically pull ink out during the appointment. Much of the fading occurs gradually after treatment as the body processes fragmented pigment.

Why are several sessions required?

Tattoo ink is deposited in layers within the deeper skin. Trying to destroy all the pigment in one appointment would increase the risk of burns, wounds, pigment changes and scarring.

Several sessions are needed because:

Only part of the pigment can be treated safely at one time

Different ink depths respond differently

The skin requires time to heal

The body needs time to clear fragmented pigment

Some colours need different laser wavelengths

Dense or layered tattoos contain more pigment

Laser treatments are therefore spaced several weeks apart. The exact interval depends on healing, tattoo characteristics and the patient’s skin response.

Can a tattoo be removed completely?

Sometimes substantial clearance is possible, but complete removal cannot be guaranteed.

Possible outcomes include:

Major fading

Partial removal

Enough lightening for a cover-up tattoo

Residual pigment

A faint “ghost” outline

Uneven clearance between colours

Visible pre-existing tattoo scarring

FDA guidance states that complete removal may take many treatments and may not be achievable in some cases.

The goal should be agreed before treatment: complete removal where realistically possible, substantial fading or preparation for a cover-up.

Which tattoos are easier to remove?

Response depends on several interacting factors.

Tattoos may respond more readily when they are:

Black or dark blue

Older

Less densely packed with ink

Amateur rather than heavily layered professional tattoos

Located in an area with good circulation

Free from significant pre-existing scarring

Dark blue and black pigments are generally easier to target than several bright or pale colours.

Even a small tattoo may require multiple sessions if the pigment is dense or deeply placed.

Which tattoos are harder to remove?

Treatment can be more difficult when the tattoo is:

Multicoloured

Recently applied

Professionally and densely layered

A cover-up over an older tattoo

Scarred

Located on the hands, feet or distant extremities

Made with resistant pigments

Cosmetic permanent makeup

White, beige or flesh-coloured

Associated with an inflammatory or allergic reaction

Published reviews note that professional and multicoloured tattoos may be particularly time-consuming and can leave residual pigment despite repeated treatment.

Which tattoo colours respond best?

Tattoo colour matters because different pigments absorb different wavelengths of light.

In general:

Black and dark blue: often respond more predictably

Red: may respond to a different wavelength from black

Green: can be more resistant and may require another laser wavelength

Yellow: often difficult to remove

White, beige and flesh tones: may darken unexpectedly

Mixed colours: may need more than one wavelength or treatment approach

The FDA notes that green, red and yellow can be more difficult than dark blue and black.

The clinic should not promise removal of every colour unless the available laser wavelengths have been documented as suitable.

What is a Q-switched Nd:YAG laser?

A Q-switched Nd:YAG laser delivers very short, high-energy pulses designed to target selected tattoo pigments while limiting unnecessary heating of the surrounding skin.

Different wavelengths from this laser family may be used for different colours. Published reviews have evaluated Q-switched Nd:YAG treatment for black and coloured tattoos across different skin phototypes, while also noting that long-term evidence and treatment standardisation remain limited.

Q-switched Nd:YAG technology is listed as available within the Aastha Skin Centre machine inventory. The device’s manufacturer, exact wavelengths and regulatory status should not be claimed until supporting records are available.

Can cosmetic tattoos and permanent makeup be removed?

Selected cosmetic tattoos may be treatable, including:

Eyebrow tattoos

Microblading pigment

Permanent eyeliner

Lip-liner tattoos

Scalp-micropigmentation areas

However, cosmetic pigment requires additional caution.

White, beige, red-brown and flesh-coloured inks may contain compounds that darken after laser exposure. What begins as a pale eyebrow or lip pigment can temporarily or permanently turn grey or black.

A test spot and staged plan may therefore be considered before treating a large cosmetic tattoo.

Can microblading be removed?

Microblading is a form of cosmetic tattooing in which pigment is placed into the skin to simulate eyebrow hairs.

Its response depends on:

Pigment composition

Colour

Depth

Number of previous sessions

Scarring

Skin tone

Whether pigments have changed colour over time

Some pigments can darken during initial laser treatment. A medical assessment and possible test spot are particularly important before treating eyebrow microblading.

Can a tattoo be lightened for a cover-up?

Yes. Some patients do not need complete tattoo removal.

Laser treatment may be used to:

Reduce the darkness of an old tattoo

Lighten dense sections

Reduce an outline

Make a future cover-up design easier

Improve the available colour options for a tattoo artist

The number of sessions needed for cover-up preparation may be lower than the number required for maximal removal.

The tattoo artist should usually wait until the treated skin has fully healed and the final degree of fading can be assessed.

Is laser tattoo removal suitable for Indian skin?

Laser tattoo removal can be performed on Indian and other medium-to-deep skin tones, but there is a greater need to protect the skin’s natural pigment.

Possible pigment-related effects include:

Temporary darkening

Temporary lightening

Prolonged uneven colour

Rare permanent pigment change

More visible post-inflammatory marks

Longer-wavelength Q-switched Nd:YAG treatment has historically been used in deeper skin tones, but conservative settings, suitable intervals and test treatment may still be needed.

The website should not describe tattoo removal as risk-free for darker skin.

Is laser tattoo removal painful?

Tattoo removal can be uncomfortable or painful.

Patients commonly describe sensations such as:

Repeated rubber-band snaps

Stinging

Heat

Pinpricks

Burning

Tenderness after treatment

The FDA and AAD both note that numbing cream or local anaesthetic may be used to reduce discomfort.

The website should not promise painless tattoo removal.

What pain-control options may be used?

Depending on the tattoo and patient, comfort measures may include:

Cooling

Topical anaesthetic

Local-anaesthetic injection

Treatment in smaller sections

Pauses during treatment

Patients should not apply high-strength numbing cream purchased online without medical guidance.

The FDA has warned about some high-concentration topical anaesthetic products marketed for tattooing and laser procedures because excessive absorption can cause seizures, irregular heart rhythm and breathing problems.

Is blistering normal after tattoo removal?

Small blisters can occur after laser tattoo removal.

Patients should:

Avoid deliberately bursting blisters

Keep the area clean

Follow the prescribed dressing plan

Avoid picking crusts

Contact the clinic if blistering is extensive

Seek review for increasing pain, pus or spreading redness

Large blisters, severe pain, skin breakdown or signs of infection require medical assessment.

Why is sun protection important?

A recent tan increases the amount of melanin competing with tattoo pigment for laser energy.

Sun exposure before or after treatment may increase the risk of:

Burns

Hyperpigmentation

Hypopigmentation

Delayed healing

Uneven results

Patients may be advised to cover the treated area with opaque clothing and use suitable sunscreen after the skin surface has healed. The AAD recommends protecting treated skin from sunlight for an extended period after tattoo-removal treatment.

Can tattoo pigment become darker after laser treatment?

Yes. Paradoxical darkening is particularly associated with some:

White pigments

Flesh-coloured pigments

Beige cosmetic inks

Pink or red-brown permanent makeup

Pigments containing certain metal compounds

The treated pigment may turn grey or black after exposure to laser energy. In some cases, the darkened pigment becomes difficult to remove.

This is one reason a test spot may be recommended for cosmetic tattoos.

What if the tattoo is itchy, raised or inflamed?

An itchy, persistently swollen or raised tattoo may represent:

Allergic pigment reaction

Infection

Granulomatous inflammation

Scar tissue

Another inflammatory skin condition

A reaction related to red or black pigment

Such tattoos should be medically assessed before laser treatment.

Fragmenting allergenic pigment can occasionally worsen an existing tattoo allergy or trigger a broader reaction, so routine removal protocols may not be appropriate.

A biopsy or alternative treatment may be considered in selected cases.

Do tattoo-removal creams work?

Tattoo-removal creams, acids and do-it-yourself kits cannot reliably reach pigment deposited in the deeper dermis.

The FDA has not approved tattoo-removal creams or DIY removal kits and reports that these products can cause:

Chemical burns

Rashes

Open wounds

Infection

Permanent scars

There is no reliable evidence that these creams safely remove tattoo pigment.

Should salt, acid or sanding be used at home?

No.

Home methods such as:

Salabrasion

Acid application

Scraping

Sanding

Cutting

Burning

Bleaching

Needling over the tattoo

can damage the skin without removing pigment safely.

Non-laser destructive methods generally carry a greater potential for scarring and unpredictable cosmetic results.

What other tattoo-removal methods exist?

Other methods include:

Surgical excision

A small tattoo may sometimes be cut out and the skin stitched together. This produces a surgical scar and is not suitable for every size or location.

Dermabrasion

The skin surface is mechanically abraded. Results can be unpredictable, and pigment may remain.

Ablative laser treatment

The upper skin layers are removed rather than selectively targeting only tattoo pigment. This can involve greater recovery and scarring risk.

The FDA notes that these methods are now less commonly used because outcomes are variable and unwanted cosmetic effects may be greater.

Can tattoo removal remove an existing tattoo scar?

No. Laser tattoo removal targets ink, not the scar created when the tattoo was originally placed.

A tattoo may already contain:

Raised scar tissue

Depressed areas

Textural change

Needle-related injury

Keloid formation

As the pigment fades, pre-existing scarring may become more visible.

Separate scar treatment may be discussed after the tattoo-removal course and complete skin healing.

Frequently asked questions

How does laser tattoo removal work?

Laser light targets tattoo pigment and breaks it into smaller particles. The body gradually clears some of those particles, causing the tattoo to fade over successive sessions.

Can a tattoo be removed completely?

Complete removal may be possible for some tattoos, but it cannot be guaranteed. Resistant colours, dense ink, cover-up layers and existing scars may leave residual pigment.

How many tattoo-removal sessions are required?

Several treatments are usually required. The number depends on colour, size, depth, density, tattoo location, skin tone and response to previous sessions.

Which tattoo colour is easiest to remove?

Black and dark-blue tattoos generally respond more predictably. Green, yellow, red, white and flesh-coloured pigments can be more difficult or require different laser wavelengths.

Is tattoo removal painful?

Tattoo removal may feel like repeated rubber-band snaps, stinging or heat. Cooling, numbing cream or local anaesthesia may be used when appropriate.

Can eyebrow microblading be removed?

Selected microblading pigments can be treated, but pale or cosmetic inks may darken after laser exposure. Assessment and a test spot may be recommended.

Is tattoo removal safe for Indian skin?

Laser treatment can be performed on Indian skin, but darkening or lightening of the natural skin colour can occur. Conservative parameters, suitable spacing and careful aftercare are important.
