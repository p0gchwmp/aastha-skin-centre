What is botulinum toxin treatment?

Botulinum toxin is a prescription injectable medicine that temporarily reduces activity in selected muscles.

In cosmetic dermatology, it may soften dynamic lines—lines that become more visible when a person frowns, smiles or raises the eyebrows. The aim is controlled relaxation rather than freezing every facial movement.

Commonly assessed concerns include:

Frown lines between the eyebrows

Forehead expression lines

Crow’s-feet around the eyes

Selected lower-face muscle concerns

Facial asymmetry related to muscle activity

The exact treatment area, dose and injection points depend on facial anatomy, muscle strength and the patient’s desired degree of correction.

What are dermal fillers?

Dermal fillers are injectable materials used to support or restore volume in selected facial areas.

Many commonly used fillers are temporary and gradually absorbed by the body. How long the effect lasts depends on the material, treatment area, tissue structure and amount used.

Dermal fillers may be considered for selected concerns involving:

Cheek volume

Chin projection or balance

Lip volume or definition

Nasolabial folds

Facial contour

Selected depressed scars

Age-related volume changes

A filler consultation should assess the entire face rather than treating one line or feature in isolation.

What is the difference between Botox and fillers?

“Botox” is a widely used brand name for one type of botulinum toxin. Botulinum toxin products and dermal fillers work differently.

Botulinum toxin

Acts on selected muscles

Mainly treats movement-related lines

Does not add facial volume

Has a temporary effect

Usually begins showing an effect within several days

Dermal fillers

Add or restore volume

Support selected contours or folds

Do not relax facial muscles

Have a duration that varies by product and area

May produce an immediately visible volume change, followed by settling

Some patients may be suitable for one treatment, both treatments or neither. Combination treatment should be planned only after medical assessment.

Who may consider botulinum toxin treatment?

Botulinum toxin may be considered by adults who are concerned about expression lines and want a temporary, non-surgical treatment.

Suitability depends on:

Facial muscle activity

Existing asymmetry

Skin condition

Age and general health

Previous injectable treatment

Medicines

Pregnancy or breastfeeding

Desired facial movement

Realistic expectations

Treatment should be individualised. The same dose and injection map should not be used automatically for every face.

Who may consider dermal fillers?

Dermal fillers may be considered when a patient has suitable facial-volume or contour concerns.

The consultation may assess:

Facial proportions

Bone structure

Soft-tissue volume

Skin quality

Lip shape

Chin and jaw balance

Previous filler

Facial movement

Medical and dental history

Risk factors for complications

Fillers are not appropriate merely because a patient wants to copy another person’s face or a filtered photograph.

What results can botulinum toxin provide?

Botulinum toxin may gradually soften selected movement-related lines while preserving natural facial expression when treatment is appropriately planned.

The AAD reports that cosmetic effects commonly begin to appear within approximately three to seven days and often last around three to four months, although timing varies between patients and treatment areas.

Possible benefits may include:

Softer frown lines

Reduced forehead creasing

Less prominent crow’s-feet

A more rested appearance

Prevention of repeated deep folding while the treatment remains active

It does not permanently stop ageing or remove every line.

Filler duration varies according to:

Product material

Facial area

Amount injected

Facial movement

Metabolism

Tissue structure

Previous treatment

Individual response

Many fillers are gradually absorbed, so repeat treatment may be needed to maintain the effect. A longer duration is not automatically better; product selection should prioritise suitability and safety.

The procedure is generally completed during a clinic visit. Most patients can return to routine activities shortly afterwards, although specific aftercare instructions should be followed.

What happens during dermal filler treatment?

A filler appointment may include:

Confirming the treatment area and product

Reviewing risks and consent

Cleaning the skin

Applying anaesthesia where appropriate

Injecting filler using a needle or cannula

Assessing symmetry and tissue response

Providing aftercare and emergency instructions

Dermal filler injection is a medical procedure. The FDA advises patients to choose a licensed healthcare professional trained and experienced in filler injection and to discuss the product and treatment site before proceeding.

Is injectable treatment painful?

Most patients experience temporary pinprick discomfort, pressure or a stinging sensation.

Comfort depends on:

Treatment area

Number of injection points

Product formulation

Anaesthesia used

Individual sensitivity

Injection technique

Some filler products contain local anaesthetic, and additional topical or local anaesthesia may be used when appropriate.

The website should not promise that injections are completely painless.

What are the common side effects of botulinum toxin?

Temporary effects can include:

Redness

Swelling

Tenderness

Bruising

Mild headache

Temporary asymmetry

Less commonly, nearby muscles may be affected, causing temporary eyelid or eyebrow drooping or unwanted weakness. Very rare symptoms can include difficulty speaking, swallowing or breathing and require urgent medical attention.

What are the risks of dermal fillers?

Common temporary effects include:

Swelling

Bruising

Redness

Tenderness

Itching

Temporary lumpiness

Less common complications can include:

Infection

Delayed inflammation

Persistent nodules

Allergic reaction

Filler movement

Open or draining wounds

A rare but serious complication can occur when filler unintentionally enters or compresses a blood vessel. This may cause tissue damage, vision problems, blindness or stroke.

Which symptoms after filler require urgent care?

Seek immediate medical assessment for:

Unusual or increasing pain

Sudden vision change

Loss of vision

White, grey, blue or mottled skin near the injection

Severe headache

Facial weakness

Difficulty speaking

Weakness or numbness

Confusion

Difficulty walking

The FDA specifically advises immediate medical care for unusual pain, visual changes, discoloured skin near the treatment area or signs of stroke during or shortly after filler treatment.

Patients should receive clear emergency contact and hospital instructions before leaving the clinic.

Who should postpone injectable treatment?

Treatment may need to be postponed or avoided when there is:

Active acne or inflamed cysts at the injection site

Skin infection

Herpes outbreak

Significant facial rash

Recent dental infection or procedure, depending on the plan

Fever or acute illness

Known allergy to the intended product

Bleeding disorder

Uncontrolled medical illness

Pregnancy or breastfeeding

Unrealistic expectations

Safety information for fillers during pregnancy and breastfeeding is limited, and cosmetic botulinum toxin treatment is generally avoided during pregnancy or lactation.

Should fillers be purchased online or self-injected?

No.

Patients should never:

Purchase injectable filler for home use

Inject themselves

Attend a filler party

Use needle-free filler pens

Accept an unidentified product

Receive treatment in a salon or home setting

The FDA warns that products sold directly to consumers may be counterfeit, contaminated or unsuitable. Needle-free filler devices are also associated with serious risks and are not considered a safe substitute for professional injection.

Can injectables look natural?

Yes, a natural-looking result may be possible when treatment is conservative and based on anatomy.

The aim may be to:

Soften a line rather than erase every crease

Restore proportion rather than overfill

Preserve facial movement

Maintain individual features

Make gradual adjustments

Avoid treating unnecessary areas

For botulinum toxin, appropriately planned treatment aims to relax selected muscles while allowing natural expression.

Can botulinum toxin and fillers be used together?

They may be used together in selected patients because they treat different components of facial ageing.

For example:

Botulinum toxin may address muscle-related lines

Filler may address volume loss or contour

Skincare may address pigmentation and texture

Laser or peel treatment may address surface concerns

Combination treatment is not necessary for everyone. The FDA notes that controlled clinical studies have not fully evaluated every filler-and-neuromodulator combination, so treatment should be individualised.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Botox and dermal fillers?

Botulinum toxin temporarily relaxes selected muscles and is mainly used for movement-related lines. Dermal fillers add or restore volume in selected facial areas.

How long does botulinum toxin last?

The cosmetic effect commonly lasts around three to four months, although duration varies by patient, dose and treatment area.

How long do dermal fillers last?

Duration varies by filler material, treatment area, amount injected and individual metabolism. Many fillers are temporary and gradually absorbed.

Will Botox make my face look frozen?

Appropriately planned treatment aims to relax selected muscles while preserving natural facial expression. The dose and injection points are adjusted according to facial movement.

Are dermal fillers safe?

Fillers can be used safely in suitable patients when an appropriate product is injected by a trained medical professional. Rare complications can be serious, including blood-vessel blockage, tissue damage and visual loss.

Can I get Botox or fillers during pregnancy?

Elective cosmetic botulinum toxin and filler treatment is generally postponed during pregnancy and breastfeeding because adequate safety information is unavailable.
