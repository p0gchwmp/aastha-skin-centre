What is botulinum toxin treatment?

Botulinum toxin is a prescription injectable medicine that temporarily reduces activity in selected muscles.

In cosmetic dermatology, it may soften dynamic lines—lines that become more visible when a person frowns, smiles or raises the eyebrows. The aim is controlled relaxation rather than freezing every facial movement.

Commonly assessed concerns include:

  • Frown lines between the eyebrows
  • Forehead expression lines
  • Crow’s-feet around the eyes
  • Selected lower-face muscle concerns
  • Facial asymmetry related to muscle activity

The exact treatment area, dose and injection points depend on facial anatomy, muscle strength and the patient’s desired degree of correction.

What is the difference between Botox and fillers?

“Botox” is a widely used brand name for one type of botulinum toxin. Botulinum toxin products and dermal fillers work differently.

What happens during botulinum toxin treatment?

A typical appointment may involve:

  • Reviewing the agreed treatment plan
  • Cleaning the skin
  • Marking selected muscle points
  • Injecting small amounts with a fine needle
  • Providing post-treatment instructions

The procedure is generally completed during a clinic visit. Most patients can return to routine activities shortly afterwards, although specific aftercare instructions should be followed.

What happens during dermal filler treatment?

A filler appointment may include:

  • Confirming the treatment area and product
  • Reviewing risks and consent
  • Cleaning the skin
  • Applying anaesthesia where appropriate
  • Injecting filler using a needle or cannula
  • Assessing symmetry and tissue response
  • Providing aftercare and emergency instructions

Dermal filler injection is a medical procedure. The FDA advises patients to choose a licensed healthcare professional trained and experienced in filler injection and to discuss the product and treatment site before proceeding.

Types, options and detailed guidance

What are dermal fillers?

Dermal fillers are injectable materials used to support or restore volume in selected facial areas.

Many commonly used fillers are temporary and gradually absorbed by the body. How long the effect lasts depends on the material, treatment area, tissue structure and amount used.

Dermal fillers may be considered for selected concerns involving:

  • Cheek volume
  • Chin projection or balance
  • Lip volume or definition
  • Nasolabial folds
  • Facial contour
  • Selected depressed scars
  • Age-related volume changes

A filler consultation should assess the entire face rather than treating one line or feature in isolation.

Botulinum toxin

  • Acts on selected muscles
  • Mainly treats movement-related lines
  • Does not add facial volume
  • Has a temporary effect
  • Usually begins showing an effect within several days

Dermal fillers

  • Add or restore volume
  • Support selected contours or folds
  • Do not relax facial muscles
  • Have a duration that varies by product and area
  • May produce an immediately visible volume change, followed by settling

Some patients may be suitable for one treatment, both treatments or neither. Combination treatment should be planned only after medical assessment.

Who may consider botulinum toxin treatment?

Botulinum toxin may be considered by adults who are concerned about expression lines and want a temporary, non-surgical treatment.

Suitability depends on:

  • Facial muscle activity
  • Existing asymmetry
  • Skin condition
  • Age and general health
  • Previous injectable treatment
  • Medicines
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Desired facial movement
  • Realistic expectations

Treatment should be individualised. The same dose and injection map should not be used automatically for every face.

Who may consider dermal fillers?

Dermal fillers may be considered when a patient has suitable facial-volume or contour concerns.

The consultation may assess:

  • Facial proportions
  • Bone structure
  • Soft-tissue volume
  • Skin quality
  • Lip shape
  • Chin and jaw balance
  • Previous filler
  • Facial movement
  • Medical and dental history
  • Risk factors for complications

Fillers are not appropriate merely because a patient wants to copy another person’s face or a filtered photograph.

What results can botulinum toxin provide?

Botulinum toxin may gradually soften selected movement-related lines while preserving natural facial expression when treatment is appropriately planned.

The AAD reports that cosmetic effects commonly begin to appear within approximately three to seven days and often last around three to four months, although timing varies between patients and treatment areas.

Possible benefits may include:

  • Softer frown lines
  • Reduced forehead creasing
  • Less prominent crow’s-feet
  • A more rested appearance
  • Prevention of repeated deep folding while the treatment remains active

It does not permanently stop ageing or remove every line.

How long do dermal fillers last?

Filler duration varies according to:

  • Product material
  • Facial area
  • Amount injected
  • Facial movement
  • Metabolism
  • Tissue structure
  • Previous treatment
  • Individual response

Many fillers are gradually absorbed, so repeat treatment may be needed to maintain the effect. A longer duration is not automatically better; product selection should prioritise suitability and safety.
