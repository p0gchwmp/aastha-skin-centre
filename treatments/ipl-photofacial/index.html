What is IPL?

IPL stands for intense pulsed light.

An IPL device produces broad-spectrum, non-coherent light rather than the single focused wavelength associated with many medical lasers. Filters and treatment settings are used to direct selected wavelengths towards targets such as:

Melanin in brown pigmentation

Oxyhaemoglobin in visible blood vessels

Selected signs of sun-related skin change

Absorbed light is converted into heat within the intended target. The body then gradually clears or remodels the treated structures.

Is IPL a laser?

No. IPL and laser treatment are both light-based procedures, but they are not identical.

IPL

Produces a range of wavelengths

Uses filters to select part of that light spectrum

Can address more than one suitable skin target

Usually treats a relatively broad surface area

Laser

Generally produces a more specific wavelength

May target one selected pigment, vessel or tissue depth

Has indications that depend on the particular laser

One treatment is not automatically superior. The appropriate choice depends on the diagnosis, skin tone, treatment target and device available.

What does “photofacial” mean?

Photofacial or photorejuvenation is a commonly used term for light-based facial treatment intended to improve selected visible signs of sun exposure, redness or uneven colour.

It does not mean that the treatment:

Replaces a surgical facelift

Removes every pigmentation concern

Permanently stops ageing

Treats every form of facial redness

Produces the same result in every patient

“Photofacial” is a broad description. The patient should be told whether the planned procedure specifically uses IPL and what concern it is intended to treat.

Which concerns may IPL photofacial address?

After examination, IPL may be considered for selected concerns including:

Persistent facial redness

Visible superficial blood vessels

Telangiectasia

Selected rosacea-related redness

Sun spots

Solar lentigines

Freckle-like superficial pigmentation

Uneven sun-related colour

Selected signs of photodamage

Mild surface-texture concerns

A systematic review involving 16 studies and 637 participants reported improvement across concerns such as telangiectasia, erythema, lentigines, hyperpigmentation and skin texture. However, nine studies were rated as having low methodological quality, so benefits should be described realistically.

Can IPL treat facial redness?

IPL may help reduce selected facial redness when dilated superficial vessels or vascular colour contribute to the concern.

A systematic review and meta-analysis involving seven studies found that IPL improved facial erythema compared with no treatment. Outcomes were broadly similar to pulsed-dye laser in the comparative studies, although the authors called for larger, higher-quality research.

Redness should be diagnosed first because it may also result from:

Rosacea

Acne

Contact dermatitis

Eczema

Sunburn

Steroid use

Infection

Lupus or another medical condition

Can IPL help rosacea?

IPL may be included in a rosacea-treatment plan for persistent facial colour, flushing or visible vessels in suitable patients.

Rosacea care may also require:

Trigger management

Gentle cleansing

Moisturiser

Daily sun protection

Prescription topical treatment

Oral treatment in selected cases

Eye assessment when ocular symptoms are present

The American Academy of Dermatology states that IPL can reduce flushing and improve texture, but it generally requires several sessions and should be combined with appropriate skincare and medical treatment when needed.

IPL controls selected visible signs; it does not permanently cure rosacea.

Can IPL treat visible facial blood vessels?

Yes, selected small superficial vessels may respond because the light can be absorbed by haemoglobin within the vessel.

Treatment may be considered for:

Telangiectasia

Fine vessels around the nose

Diffuse cheek redness

Selected post-inflammatory vascular marks

Sun-related vascular changes

In a randomised split-face study, IPL improved telangiectasia and irregular pigmentation, although the improvement was mainly mild or moderate and one scar was reported among the participants.

Large, deep or blue vessels may require another vascular treatment.

Can IPL treat brown spots?

IPL may help selected superficial brown spots that contain excess melanin, including some sun-related lentigines and freckle-like pigmentation.

The response depends on:

Type of pigmentation

Depth of pigment

Skin tone

Recent sun exposure

Treatment filter and settings

Presence of melasma

Tendency to develop dark marks

Brown spots should be examined before treatment. A changing, irregular, bleeding or unusual lesion should not be treated cosmetically until a dermatologist has excluded a suspicious growth.

Is IPL suitable for melasma?

IPL is not a routine first treatment for every patient with melasma.

Melasma is chronic, frequently recurs and is particularly common in people with medium-to-deep skin tones. Although IPL has been studied as part of combination therapy, results are variable and heat or inflammation can worsen pigmentation in some patients.

Treatment usually begins with:

Diagnosis confirmation

Broad-spectrum sunscreen

Visible-light protection where appropriate

Prescription depigmenting treatment

Trigger management

Long-term maintenance

Can IPL treat post-acne marks?

IPL may help when post-acne marks contain a combination of superficial brown pigmentation or persistent vascular redness.

It does not treat every post-acne concern equally.

It may support treatment for

Selected red post-acne marks

Superficial brown marks

Uneven colour after acne

It does not adequately treat

Deep rolling scars

Boxcar scars

Ice-pick scars

Tethered scars

Active nodular acne

People with darker skin tones have a greater tendency to develop post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, and light-based treatment itself can occasionally worsen it.

Can IPL improve wrinkles or loose skin?

IPL may improve selected aspects of photodamaged skin, particularly uneven pigmentation, superficial vessels and texture.

Its effect on deeper wrinkles or skin laxity is limited. In one randomised controlled trial, IPL improved texture, telangiectasia and irregular pigmentation but did not produce significant improvement in wrinkles.

IPL does not replace:

Botulinum toxin for expression lines

Dermal fillers for volume loss

HIFU or RF for selected laxity

Fractional resurfacing for deeper texture

Surgical lifting for substantial loose skin

Is IPL the same as laser hair reduction?

No. IPL can be used for hair reduction with suitable devices and protocols, but an IPL photofacial uses different goals, filters, settings and treatment planning.

A photofacial page should not claim permanent hair removal.

Patients seeking unwanted-hair treatment should receive a separate assessment.

Is IPL the same as LED therapy?

No.

IPL

Uses high-intensity pulses

Produces controlled heating

Targets pigment or blood vessels

Requires eye protection

Can cause burns or pigment changes

LED or photobiomodulation

Uses much lower-intensity light

Does not use the same target-destruction mechanism

Has different indications and risks

The two terms should not be used interchangeably.

Why does skin tone matter?

IPL energy can be absorbed by both the intended pigment and the normal melanin in surrounding skin.

When the skin contains more melanin, there may be a higher risk of:

Excessive heat absorption

Burns

Blistering

Post-inflammatory darkening

Loss of natural skin colour

Uneven results

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation is more common and can be more persistent in darker skin tones.

This does not mean IPL is impossible for every Indian patient. It means diagnosis, device selection, conservative settings and aftercare require particular care.

Why should IPL not be performed on recently tanned skin?

A tan increases melanin within the skin, creating greater competition between normal skin pigment and the intended treatment target.

This can raise the risk of:

Burns

Blisters

Dark marks

Light patches

Uneven response

FDA guidance for high-energy light procedures advises particular caution in patients who are tanned, planning significant sun exposure, prone to dark marks or taking photosensitising medicines.

Treatment may be postponed until the tan has faded.

What happens during an IPL photofacial?

A typical appointment may involve:

Confirming and photographing the treatment area

Cleansing the skin

Covering or excluding unsuitable lesions

Providing protective eyewear

Applying cooling or conductive gel where required

Selecting an appropriate filter and settings

Delivering controlled pulses across planned areas

Monitoring the skin response after each pulse

Cooling or soothing the treated skin

Applying moisturiser and sunscreen

Providing written aftercare instructions

IPL is a high-powered light treatment, so appropriate patient and operator eye protection is essential. FDA-recognised standards specifically address protective eyewear for intense-light procedures.

What does IPL feel like?

Patients may feel:

Bright flashes behind the eye protection

Warmth

Brief stinging

A snapping sensation

Pinprick discomfort

Heat over darker spots

Temporary tenderness

Discomfort varies with the area, settings, cooling and individual sensitivity.

A clinical trial reported treatment-related pain significant enough for three participants to withdraw, illustrating why the procedure should not be promoted as completely painless.

Is anaesthesia required?

Many IPL photofacial treatments are performed without injectable anaesthesia.

Comfort measures may include:

Contact cooling

Cooling gel

Cold-air support

Treatment-setting adjustment

Smaller treatment sections

Topical anaesthesia when appropriate for the device and indication

The patient should immediately report sharp pain, intense focal heat or burning.

Is there downtime after IPL?

Many patients return to ordinary activities after treatment, but temporary skin changes are common.

These may include:

Redness

Mild swelling

Warmth

Tenderness

Temporary darkening of brown spots

Fine crusting

Mild bruising around vessels

Dryness or sensitivity

The degree of downtime depends on the condition treated, skin tone, treatment energy and individual response.

How many IPL sessions are needed?

IPL is usually performed as a treatment course rather than a guaranteed one-session procedure.

The schedule depends on:

Main treatment target

Severity

Skin tone

Device and filter

Initial response

Treatment tolerance

Healing between sessions

Maintenance needs

In the skin-rejuvenation systematic review, patients received an average of approximately four sessions, with included protocols ranging from three to seven. This does not mean every patient needs the same number.

When do results become visible?

Some vascular or pigmented targets may begin changing after one session, but final improvement develops gradually.

Patients may notice:

Reduced diffuse redness

Fading of selected superficial vessels

Darkening followed by fading of suitable brown spots

More even-looking colour

Mild improvement in texture

Several appointments may be needed before the overall response can be assessed.

Frequently asked questions

What is an IPL photofacial?

An IPL photofacial uses broad-spectrum pulses of light to target selected pigment or superficial blood vessels in the skin. It may be used for suitable redness, telangiectasia and sun-related pigmentation concerns.

Is IPL the same as laser treatment?

No. IPL produces a broad range of filtered wavelengths, while a laser generally produces a more specific wavelength. Their uses and risks depend on the device and diagnosis.

Can IPL help rosacea?

IPL may reduce persistent facial redness and visible vessels in selected rosacea patients. It does not permanently cure rosacea and may be combined with skincare, trigger management and medicine.

Can IPL remove pigmentation?

IPL may improve selected superficial sun spots or freckle-like pigmentation. It is not appropriate for every pigment disorder, and melasma may worsen in unsuitable patients.

Is IPL safe for Indian skin?

IPL can be considered for selected Indian patients, but medium-to-deep skin has a greater risk of burns and post-inflammatory pigmentation. Conservative settings and careful patient selection are important.

Is IPL painful?

Patients may feel warmth, snapping or pinprick discomfort. Cooling can help, but treatment should not be described as completely painless.

How many IPL sessions are needed?

A course of treatment is commonly required. Published photorejuvenation studies have often used three to seven sessions, but the individual schedule depends on the concern and response.
