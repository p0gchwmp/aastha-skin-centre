What is a Hydrafacial?

“Hydrafacial” is commonly used to describe a hydradermabrasion-style facial procedure involving several steps, which may include:

Cleansing

Controlled exfoliation

Suction-assisted extraction

Application of hydrating solutions

Application of selected serums

Optional supportive steps according to the skin concern

Hydradermabrasion combines crystal-free surface exfoliation with pneumatic or suction-assisted application of skincare solutions. A small controlled study reported improvements in selected measures of skin quality after a course of treatment, although only 20 women participated.

The exact device, tips, solutions and sequence can vary. The clinic should therefore avoid publishing one branded protocol unless the exact system used has been documented.

What is a medifacial?

A medifacial, or medical facial, is a broad term for a customised clinic-based facial planned according to the patient’s skin type and concern.

Depending on assessment, it may include a combination of:

Gentle cleansing

Enzyme or superficial exfoliation

Comedone care

Hydradermabrasion

Controlled suction

Hydrating masks

Suitable active serums

LED treatment where clinically appropriate

Moisturiser and sunscreen

There is no single universal medifacial protocol. A facial for dry, sensitive skin should not be identical to one designed for oily or congested skin.

How is a medifacial different from a salon facial?

A dermatologist-led medifacial begins with assessment of the skin rather than selection from a standard beauty menu.

The consultation may consider:

Active acne

Skin sensitivity

Rosacea

Eczema or dermatitis

Pigmentation tendency

Current medicines

Retinoid use

Recent peels or lasers

Pregnancy or breastfeeding

History of cold sores

Upcoming events

The aim is to avoid ingredients or steps that may aggravate an existing skin disorder. A facial should not be described as “medical” merely because stronger products are used.

A facial cannot diagnose or permanently correct every concern in this list.

What does current research show?

Evidence for hydradermabrasion is more limited than the evidence available for many established medical treatments.

Published studies include:

A 20-participant trial reporting changes in fine lines, pore appearance, pigmentation and microscopic skin features after six hydradermabrasion sessions

An imaging study involving only eight volunteers

A 20-patient, open-label study of a branded clarifying protocol for mild-to-moderate acne

The acne study had no blinded control group, and an author was affiliated with the device company. These limitations mean results should be interpreted cautiously rather than promoted as proof that the treatment cures acne or permanently changes the skin.

Can Hydrafacial help acne?

A hydradermabrasion or clarifying facial may support selected patients with:

Mild comedonal acne

Blackheads

Whiteheads

Excess surface oil

Congested pores

Rough texture

A small open-label study involving 20 adults with mild-to-moderate acne reported improvement after six treatments over 12 weeks. However, the study was small, unblinded and included company involvement, so the procedure should be viewed as supportive care rather than a replacement for established acne treatment.

Inflammatory, nodular, cystic or scarring acne usually requires a dermatologist-directed medical plan.

Can it remove blackheads?

Suction and controlled exfoliation may temporarily clear some superficial pore contents and improve the appearance of congestion.

However:

Not every dark pore is a blackhead

Deep comedones may need separate extraction

Aggressive suction can irritate sensitive skin

Blackheads can return

Ongoing acne care may still be required

Repeated strong extraction should not be performed over inflamed acne, broken skin or fragile blood vessels.

Can it improve dry or dehydrated-looking skin?

Selected hydrating solutions and moisturising steps may improve the temporary appearance and feel of dry or dehydrated skin.

Patients may notice:

Softer-feeling skin

Temporary plumping

Smoother surface texture

Improved visible radiance

Better comfort after moisturising

Small studies of hydradermabrasion and serum-assisted microdermabrasion have reported short-term improvements in hydration and skin appearance, but protocols, products and funding sources vary.

Persistent dryness may instead result from eczema, irritation, over-exfoliation, medicines or an unsuitable skincare routine.

Can Hydrafacial treat pigmentation or melasma?

A superficial facial may temporarily improve overall brightness or help selected superficial dark marks appear less prominent.

It is not a stand-alone cure for:

Melasma

Deep pigmentation

Hormonal pigmentation

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation

Freckles

Sun-induced lentigines

AAD guidance notes that controlled microdermabrasion may support treatment of uneven tone, superficial dark marks and melasma in selected patients, generally as one part of a broader plan.

Melasma usually requires consistent sunscreen, suitable prescription skincare and long-term maintenance.

Can a facial remove acne scars?

No routine Hydrafacial or medifacial can remove deep acne scars.

It may temporarily improve:

Surface smoothness

Dryness around scars

Congestion

Post-acne dullness

Superficial dark marks

Rolling, boxcar, ice-pick and tethered scars may require procedures such as:

MNRF

Microneedling

Subcision

TCA CROSS

Fractional CO₂ laser

Combination scar treatment

Can it reduce pores permanently?

No treatment can permanently open, close or erase pores.

A facial may make pores appear temporarily less prominent by:

Removing some surface debris

Reducing congestion

Improving hydration

Smoothing the outer skin surface

Controlling excess oil as part of a wider plan

Pore appearance is also influenced by genetics, oil production, acne, skin elasticity and sun-related change.

Can Hydrafacial remove wrinkles?

Hydration and surface exfoliation may temporarily soften the appearance of very fine dehydration lines.

The treatment cannot:

Remove deep wrinkles

Relax expression muscles

Restore substantial facial volume

Lift significantly loose skin

Replace botulinum toxin

Replace fillers

Replace resurfacing procedures

An early small study reported improvements in fine-line appearance after a treatment course, but the evidence is not strong enough to promise wrinkle removal.

What happens during the treatment?

A customised appointment may involve:

Cleansing the skin

Examining the face under appropriate lighting

Performing controlled exfoliation

Applying a mild solution when suitable

Using suction-based cleansing or extraction

Applying selected hydrating or supportive serums

Using a calming mask or optional supportive treatment

Applying moisturiser

Not every patient requires every step. Stronger or longer treatment is not automatically better.

What does the procedure feel like?

Patients may feel:

Coolness

Light suction

A wet or swirling sensation

Mild pressure

Gentle scratching or exfoliation

Temporary tingling from selected solutions

Controlled microdermabrasion is generally described as non-invasive, although temporary sensitivity can occur.

The treatment should not be advertised as completely sensation-free or painless for every patient.

Is there downtime?

Many patients resume ordinary activities after a superficial facial.

Temporary effects may include:

Mild redness

Tightness

Dryness

Temporary sensitivity

Mild swelling

Tingling

A short acne flare

Irritation from an active solution

AAD guidance for controlled microdermabrasion notes that redness or slight swelling can occur and may resemble mild windburn or sunburn for a day or two.

What complications are possible?

Possible complications include:

Irritation

Excessive redness

Burning

Bruising from suction

Broken superficial blood vessels

Acne flare

Contact dermatitis

Cold-sore reactivation

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation

Infection from poor hygiene

Aggravation of rosacea or eczema

People who tend to develop dark marks after inflammation may have a greater risk of post-procedure pigmentation.

The risk depends on skin sensitivity, products used, suction strength, technique and aftercare.

Who should postpone treatment?

A facial may need to be postponed or modified when there is:

Active bacterial, viral or fungal infection

Open wounds

Active cold sores

Sunburn

Significant dermatitis

A severe rosacea flare

Painful cystic acne

Recent facial surgery

A suspicious or changing skin growth

Recent strong peel or laser treatment

Severe skin-barrier damage

Known allergy to a planned ingredient

The AAD advises dermatologist assessment before microdermabrasion when a person has a growing or bleeding spot, scars easily or has used isotretinoin recently.

What about isotretinoin and acne medicines?

Patients should disclose:

Current isotretinoin use

Previous isotretinoin treatment

Prescription retinoids

Benzoyl peroxide

Salicylic acid

Glycolic acid

Antibiotic treatment

Other exfoliating medicines or skincare

AAD guidance warns that people taking or recently taking isotretinoin may have an increased complication risk from microdermabrasion and may need to delay treatment.

Products should only be paused when advised by the treating clinician.

Can treatment be performed during pregnancy?

A gentle facial may be possible during pregnancy, but the exact ingredients matter.

Some protocols may contain:

Salicylic acid

Retinoid-related ingredients

Strong exfoliating acids

Unverified boosters

Essential oils or fragrances

LED or device-based additions

Patients should tell the clinic that they are pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive so that the treatment can be modified or postponed.

No facial should be labelled “pregnancy safe” without reviewing every product and treatment step.

Is it suitable for sensitive or rosacea-prone skin?

Some sensitive-skin patients may tolerate a simplified hydrating facial, while others may become more inflamed after suction, exfoliation or active serums.

Treatment may need to:

Avoid aggressive suction

Avoid fragrance

Avoid strong acids

Exclude inflamed areas

Use fewer steps

Focus on barrier support

Be postponed during an active flare

A red or burning face should be diagnosed before it is treated as simple “sensitivity.”

Is it suitable for Indian skin?

A carefully selected superficial facial may be used across different skin tones.

However, Indian and deeper skin tones can develop post-inflammatory pigmentation after:

Excessive suction

Scrubbing

Burns

Strong acids

Picking or extraction trauma

Treatment over active inflammation

AAD guidance states that dermatologist-performed microdermabrasion can be used across skin colours, while also noting that people prone to dark marks require particular care.

How many sessions may be required?

A single facial may provide temporary hydration or visible radiance. A course may be considered when treating ongoing congestion or texture concerns.

The schedule depends on:

Skin concern

Sensitivity

Products used

Response to the first session

Other procedures

AAD guidance notes that microdermabrasion commonly requires a series of treatments and that results are temporary, with repeat sessions often used for maintenance.

Patients should not be committed to a large prepaid package before the skin’s response is known.

How long do the results last?

Visible hydration and radiance may be noticed soon after treatment, but the effect is temporary.

Duration depends on:

Skin type

Climate

Can treatment be done before a wedding or event?

A facial is often planned before an event for temporary hydration and smoother-looking skin.

However, first-time treatment should not be scheduled immediately before an important occasion because unexpected redness, irritation, acne flare or allergy can occur.

A safer plan may include:

Consultation several weeks earlier

A trial treatment

Avoiding aggressive extraction

Avoiding unfamiliar active ingredients

Maintaining a stable skincare routine

Allowing time for any reaction to settle

The final timing should be based on skin sensitivity and treatment intensity.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Hydrafacial?

A Hydrafacial-style treatment combines surface exfoliation, suction-assisted cleansing and application of selected hydrating solutions. The exact steps and products vary according to the system and patient’s skin.

What is the difference between Hydrafacial and medifacial?

Hydrafacial commonly refers to a device-assisted hydradermabrasion treatment. Medifacial is a broader term for a customised clinic facial that may include hydradermabrasion, exfoliation, masks, serums or other suitable steps.

Can Hydrafacial treat acne?

It may support selected mild comedonal or congested acne. It does not replace prescription treatment for inflammatory, nodular, cystic or scarring acne.

Does Hydrafacial remove blackheads?

Suction and exfoliation may remove some superficial pore debris and blackheads temporarily. Ongoing acne or oil-control treatment may still be required.

Is Hydrafacial painful?

Most patients feel light suction, coolness, pressure or tingling. Sensitive skin can experience temporary discomfort or irritation, so completely painless treatment cannot be promised.

Is there downtime after a medifacial?

Many patients resume routine activities immediately, but temporary redness, tightness, swelling or sensitivity can occur for a day or two.

How often should I get a Hydrafacial?

Frequency depends on skin type, concern, products used and response. Some patients choose occasional treatment, while others may undergo a planned course followed by maintenance.
