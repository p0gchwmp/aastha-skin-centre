What is white or grey hair?

Hair receives its colour from melanin produced within the hair follicle.

Hair becomes grey or white when the follicle produces less melanin or stops producing it. Grey hair retains some pigment, while completely white hair has very little or no visible melanin in the hair shaft.

White hair may appear on the:

  • Scalp
  • Eyebrows
  • Beard
  • Moustache
  • Chin
  • Upper lip
  • Cheeks
  • Neck
  • Chest
  • Arms
  • Other body areas

What is electrolysis?

Electrolysis treats one hair follicle at a time.

A very fine sterile probe is inserted into the follicular opening. Electrical energy is then delivered to damage the hair-producing structures, after which the loosened hair is removed.

Unlike laser treatment, electrolysis does not depend on the hair containing melanin. It can therefore be used for:

  • White hair
  • Grey hair
  • Blonde hair
  • Red hair
  • Dark hair
  • Fine isolated hairs
  • Mixed-colour facial hair
Types, options and detailed guidance

What does “white hair removal” mean on this page?

This page concerns the removal of unwanted white or grey facial and body hair.

It does not refer primarily to:

  • Reversing grey scalp hair
  • Dyeing scalp or beard hair
  • Treating hair loss
  • Restoring pigment to vitiligo-affected hair
  • Hair transplantation

A patient seeking treatment for premature greying may need a separate hair-and-scalp assessment rather than a hair-removal procedure.

Why does unwanted facial hair turn white?

  • Facial hair can lose pigment because of:
  • Normal ageing
  • Family or genetic tendency
  • Premature greying
  • Reduced follicular melanin production
  • Vitiligo or poliosis
  • Previous inflammation
  • Selected medicines or medical conditions
  • Hair growing back after earlier dark-hair reduction while residual white hairs remain

Age-related greying is common and is not usually a disease.

Does white facial hair mean something is medically wrong?

Usually not.

A few white chin or facial hairs commonly develop with age. Medical assessment becomes more relevant when the change is:

  • Very early in life
  • Sudden or rapidly progressive
  • Associated with hair loss
  • Confined to a white skin patch
  • Accompanied by fatigue or weight change
  • Associated with irregular periods or severe acne
  • Accompanied by rapidly increasing coarse facial hair
  • Linked with another autoimmune or nutritional concern

The decision to investigate depends on the full clinical picture rather than the colour of one or two hairs.

Can white hair become naturally dark again?

Established age-related white hair usually does not reliably regain its original colour.

Premature greying has been associated in some studies with family history, smoking, thyroid disorders and selected nutritional deficiencies, but the evidence is inconsistent and treatment does not guarantee repigmentation. Supplements should be used only when a genuine deficiency or medical indication is identified.

Removal and repigmentation are different goals. A treatment that removes a white hair does not restore its colour.

Why does conventional laser hair reduction not work on white hair?

Hair-removal lasers work through selective photothermolysis.

Laser energy is absorbed by melanin in a dark hair shaft and transferred to the follicle. White hair lacks enough pigment to absorb the required energy, so the laser cannot reliably distinguish it from the surrounding skin.

The American Academy of Dermatology specifically notes that laser hair removal does not work effectively on white, grey, blonde or red hair.

Can a stronger laser setting remove white hair?

No reliable benefit should be expected simply from increasing the energy.

  • Using excessive energy when the hair lacks a suitable pigment target can increase the risk of:
  • Burns
  • Blisters
  • Crusting
  • Infection
  • Dark pigmentation
  • Light pigmentation
  • Scarring

The limitation is the absence of pigment within the hair—not merely insufficient machine power.

Can diode or Nd:YAG laser remove white hair?

Diode and long-pulsed Nd:YAG lasers can reduce suitable pigmented hair when the correct patient, skin type and settings are selected.

They still depend on a pigment target within the hair. A fully white hair generally cannot be treated effectively merely by changing from one conventional hair-removal wavelength to another.

Can IPL remove white or grey hair?

IPL also relies largely on pigment within the hair shaft.

It is therefore generally ineffective for completely white hair. Treating pigment-free hair repeatedly with IPL can expose the surrounding skin to heat without producing meaningful follicular reduction.

Experimental methods that add an external chromophore or photosensitiser have been studied, but available research is limited and has not established a routine, dependable white-hair removal protocol.

Is electrolysis permanent?

Electrolysis is recognised as a method capable of permanently removing successfully treated individual follicles.

  • However, several practical limitations remain:
  • Every follicle must be treated separately
  • Not every hair is visible or treatable during one session
  • Hair grows in cycles
  • Some follicles may require repeat treatment
  • New hormonally influenced hairs may develop later
  • Results depend heavily on operator technique

Several sessions are therefore needed even when the eventual goal is permanent removal of the treated hairs.

Why are multiple electrolysis sessions needed?

Hair follicles pass through active growth, transition and resting phases.

A session only treats hairs that are present and accessible at that time. Other follicles may produce visible hairs during later weeks or months.

The total treatment period depends on:

  • Number of hairs
  • Body area
  • Hair-growth cycle
  • Hair thickness
  • Hormonal influences
  • Previous waxing or plucking
  • Session length
  • Skin response
  • Consistency of appointments

No fixed session count should be promised before the area has been assessed.

Is electrolysis suitable for Indian skin?

Electrolysis can be performed across different skin tones because it does not rely on colour contrast between the skin and hair.

Indian and deeper skin tones can nevertheless develop:

  • Temporary redness
  • Small crusts
  • Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation
  • Light spots
  • Burns
  • Infection
  • Scarring
  • Keloids in susceptible patients

The AAD advises choosing an appropriately trained professional because poor technique or unsterile probes can cause burns, scars and infection.

Frequently asked questions

Can black and white hairs be treated together?

Yes, but different methods may be required.

Can white facial hair be shaved?

Yes.

Can white hair be plucked or tweezed?

Plucking removes an individual hair from the follicle temporarily.

Can threading or waxing remove white hair?

Yes, both methods remove the hair mechanically and do not depend on pigment.

Are hair-removal creams suitable?

Chemical depilatories dissolve hair near the skin surface.

Can home IPL devices remove white hair?

No reliable white-hair reduction should be expected from a home IPL device.
