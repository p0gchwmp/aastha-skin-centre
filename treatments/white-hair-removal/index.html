What is white or grey hair?

Hair receives its colour from melanin produced within the hair follicle.

Hair becomes grey or white when the follicle produces less melanin or stops producing it. Grey hair retains some pigment, while completely white hair has very little or no visible melanin in the hair shaft.

White hair may appear on the:

Scalp

Eyebrows

Beard

Moustache

Chin

Upper lip

Cheeks

Neck

Chest

Arms

Other body areas

What does “white hair removal” mean on this page?

This page concerns the removal of unwanted white or grey facial and body hair.

It does not refer primarily to:

Reversing grey scalp hair

Dyeing scalp or beard hair

Treating hair loss

Restoring pigment to vitiligo-affected hair

Hair transplantation

A patient seeking treatment for premature greying may need a separate hair-and-scalp assessment rather than a hair-removal procedure.

Why does unwanted facial hair turn white?

Facial hair can lose pigment because of:

Normal ageing

Family or genetic tendency

Premature greying

Reduced follicular melanin production

Vitiligo or poliosis

Previous inflammation

Selected medicines or medical conditions

Hair growing back after earlier dark-hair reduction while residual white hairs remain

Age-related greying is common and is not usually a disease.

Does white facial hair mean something is medically wrong?

Usually not.

A few white chin or facial hairs commonly develop with age. Medical assessment becomes more relevant when the change is:

Very early in life

Sudden or rapidly progressive

Associated with hair loss

Confined to a white skin patch

Accompanied by fatigue or weight change

Associated with irregular periods or severe acne

Accompanied by rapidly increasing coarse facial hair

Linked with another autoimmune or nutritional concern

The decision to investigate depends on the full clinical picture rather than the colour of one or two hairs.

Can white hair become naturally dark again?

Established age-related white hair usually does not reliably regain its original colour.

Premature greying has been associated in some studies with family history, smoking, thyroid disorders and selected nutritional deficiencies, but the evidence is inconsistent and treatment does not guarantee repigmentation. Supplements should be used only when a genuine deficiency or medical indication is identified.

Removal and repigmentation are different goals. A treatment that removes a white hair does not restore its colour.

Why does conventional laser hair reduction not work on white hair?

Hair-removal lasers work through selective photothermolysis.

Laser energy is absorbed by melanin in a dark hair shaft and transferred to the follicle. White hair lacks enough pigment to absorb the required energy, so the laser cannot reliably distinguish it from the surrounding skin.

The American Academy of Dermatology specifically notes that laser hair removal does not work effectively on white, grey, blonde or red hair.

Can a stronger laser setting remove white hair?

No reliable benefit should be expected simply from increasing the energy.

Using excessive energy when the hair lacks a suitable pigment target can increase the risk of:

Burns

Blisters

Crusting

Infection

Dark pigmentation

Light pigmentation

Scarring

The limitation is the absence of pigment within the hair—not merely insufficient machine power.

Can diode or Nd:YAG laser remove white hair?

Diode and long-pulsed Nd:YAG lasers can reduce suitable pigmented hair when the correct patient, skin type and settings are selected.

They still depend on a pigment target within the hair. A fully white hair generally cannot be treated effectively merely by changing from one conventional hair-removal wavelength to another.

Laser hair reduction is confirmed at both Aastha branches, but it should be used only for suitable pigmented hairs.

Can IPL remove white or grey hair?

IPL also relies largely on pigment within the hair shaft.

It is therefore generally ineffective for completely white hair. Treating pigment-free hair repeatedly with IPL can expose the surrounding skin to heat without producing meaningful follicular reduction.

Experimental methods that add an external chromophore or photosensitiser have been studied, but available research is limited and has not established a routine, dependable white-hair removal protocol.

What is electrolysis?

Electrolysis treats one hair follicle at a time.

A very fine sterile probe is inserted into the follicular opening. Electrical energy is then delivered to damage the hair-producing structures, after which the loosened hair is removed.

Unlike laser treatment, electrolysis does not depend on the hair containing melanin. It can therefore be used for:

White hair

Grey hair

Blonde hair

Red hair

Dark hair

Fine isolated hairs

Mixed-colour facial hair

Is electrolysis permanent?

Electrolysis is recognised as a method capable of permanently removing successfully treated individual follicles.

However, several practical limitations remain:

Every follicle must be treated separately

Not every hair is visible or treatable during one session

Hair grows in cycles

Some follicles may require repeat treatment

New hormonally influenced hairs may develop later

Results depend heavily on operator technique

Several sessions are therefore needed even when the eventual goal is permanent removal of the treated hairs.

Why are multiple electrolysis sessions needed?

Hair follicles pass through active growth, transition and resting phases.

A session only treats hairs that are present and accessible at that time. Other follicles may produce visible hairs during later weeks or months.

The total treatment period depends on:

Number of hairs

Body area

Hair-growth cycle

Hair thickness

Hormonal influences

Previous waxing or plucking

Session length

Skin response

Consistency of appointments

No fixed session count should be promised before the area has been assessed.

Is electrolysis suitable for Indian skin?

Electrolysis can be performed across different skin tones because it does not rely on colour contrast between the skin and hair.

Indian and deeper skin tones can nevertheless develop:

Temporary redness

Small crusts

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation

Light spots

Burns

Infection

Scarring

Keloids in susceptible patients

The AAD advises choosing an appropriately trained professional because poor technique or unsterile probes can cause burns, scars and infection.

Can black and white hairs be treated together?

Yes, but different methods may be required.

A mixed-hair plan may involve:

Laser hair reduction for suitable dark, coarse hairs

Electrolysis for residual white or grey hairs

Temporary trimming or shaving between sessions when advised

Medical treatment when ongoing hormonal hair growth is present

Laser treatment should not be counted as a white-hair treatment merely because dark and white hairs are present in the same area.

Can white facial hair be shaved?

Yes.

Shaving cuts the hair at the skin surface. It does not:

Increase the number of follicles

Make the hair biologically thicker

Turn nearby hair white

Make hair grow faster

Change the follicle’s pigment production

The blunt cut end may feel coarser while it grows out, but the underlying hair has not changed.

Shaving is a reasonable temporary option when the skin tolerates it.

Can white hair be plucked or tweezed?

Plucking removes an individual hair from the follicle temporarily.

Possible disadvantages include:

Pain

Folliculitis

Ingrown hair

Red bumps

Pigmentation

Small scars

Difficulty identifying hairs for electrolysis

Repeated skin trauma

Frequent plucking does not create more hair, but it may complicate a planned electrolysis schedule because the hair must be visible and connected with the follicle for treatment.

Can threading or waxing remove white hair?

Yes, both methods remove the hair mechanically and do not depend on pigment.

Their effects are temporary. Possible problems include:

Irritation

Folliculitis

Skin lifting

Burns from hot wax

Ingrown hair

Post-inflammatory pigmentation

Contact allergy to wax or products

Worsening of active acne or dermatitis

Waxing should generally be avoided immediately before electrolysis or laser assessment.

Are hair-removal creams suitable?

Chemical depilatories dissolve hair near the skin surface.

They may remove white hair temporarily, but sensitive facial skin can develop:

Burning

Irritant dermatitis

Allergic contact dermatitis

Swelling

Dark pigmentation

Chemical injury

A patch test should be performed according to the product instructions. Products should not be used near the eyes, on broken skin or on areas for which the manufacturer has not approved their use.

Can home IPL devices remove white hair?

No reliable white-hair reduction should be expected from a home IPL device.

These devices also depend on light absorption by pigment. A device that cannot detect sufficient contrast may refuse to fire, while unsuitable use can still irritate or burn the surrounding skin.

Patients should not repeatedly increase settings in an attempt to make white hair respond.

When should excess facial hair be medically evaluated?

Medical assessment may be appropriate when a woman develops:

Rapidly increasing coarse facial hair

Irregular or absent periods

Severe or persistent acne

Scalp-hair thinning

Voice deepening

Increased muscularity

Unexplained weight change

Dark, velvety neck skin

Fertility concerns

Sudden hair growth after menopause

Hirsutism may be associated with PCOS or another androgen-related condition. Endocrine guidance recommends considering androgen testing when hair growth is clinically significant, rapidly progressive or accompanied by menstrual or other endocrine abnormalities.

The hair may become white with age, but the reason it is growing can still require evaluation.

Does every woman with a few chin hairs need hormone tests?

No.

Routine androgen testing is not generally recommended for a woman with a few local unwanted hairs, regular periods and no other evidence of an endocrine disorder.

Testing becomes more relevant when hair growth is widespread, progressive or accompanied by menstrual disturbance, severe acne, scalp-hair loss or other signs of androgen excess.

What if white hair appears inside a white skin patch?

White hair occurring within a depigmented skin patch may represent leukotrichia associated with vitiligo or another local pigment disorder.

The skin should be examined for:

Milky-white patches

Sharply defined pigment loss

White eyebrow or eyelash hair

Previous inflammation

Scarring

Other areas of pigment change

Vitiligo can cause hair within an affected area to become white or grey.

What are the possible risks of electrolysis?

Possible treatment effects include:

Stinging or discomfort

Temporary redness

Mild swelling

Pinpoint crusts

Folliculitis

Post-inflammatory pigmentation

Light pigmentation

Burns

Infection

Scarring

Keloid formation

Incomplete hair destruction

Sterile probes, correct energy settings and appropriate technique reduce risk but cannot eliminate it completely.

Is electrolysis painful?

Each treated hair may produce a brief stinging, heating or pinching sensation.

Discomfort varies according to:

Body area

Hair thickness

Treatment current

Session duration

Individual pain sensitivity

Skin inflammation

Operator technique

Sensitive facial areas may feel more uncomfortable than less sensitive body areas. Pain-free treatment should not be guaranteed.

Who may need treatment postponed or modified?

Treatment may be postponed or adjusted when there is:

Active bacterial or fungal infection

Herpes outbreak

Open skin

Severe acne inflammation

Sunburn

Active eczema

Recent waxing or plucking

Poorly controlled diabetes

Reduced immunity

A bleeding disorder

A pacemaker or implanted electronic device requiring medical review

A strong history of keloids

Pregnancy-related procedural restrictions

Every medicine, implant and relevant medical condition should be disclosed before treatment.

How should patients prepare?

Depending on the chosen method, instructions may include:

Allowing the hairs to become visible

Avoiding waxing or plucking

Trimming rather than pulling hairs out

Avoiding strong acids or retinoids near the area

Treating active infection first

Arriving with clean skin

Avoiding tanning and sunburn

Sharing previous laser or electrolysis history

Reporting keloid or pigmentation problems

Following any individual pre-procedure instructions

The hair should usually be long enough for the operator to identify and remove after the follicle is treated.

What aftercare may be needed?

After electrolysis or another follicular procedure, patients may be advised to:

Clean the area gently

Avoid touching with unwashed hands

Avoid picking small crusts

Use a prescribed soothing product

Avoid waxing or threading the treated area

Avoid heavy makeup temporarily

Avoid excessive heat and sweating

Use sunscreen on exposed skin

Report increasing pain, pus or spreading redness

Attend review if pigmentation or scarring develops

The exact aftercare depends on the treatment method and body area.

Frequently asked questions

Can laser remove completely white hair?

Conventional laser hair reduction generally cannot remove fully white hair because the hair lacks enough melanin to absorb the laser energy.

What is the suitable procedure for white facial hair?

Electrolysis is the established direct-removal method for white and other light-coloured hairs because it treats each follicle electrically rather than relying on hair pigment.

Is electrolysis permanent?

Electrolysis can permanently remove successfully treated individual follicles, but multiple sessions are required because hairs grow in cycles and not every follicle is treatable during one visit.

How many electrolysis sessions will I need?

The number depends on hair density, treatment area, growth cycle, hormonal factors and appointment consistency. A fixed number cannot be predicted without examining the area.

Is electrolysis safe for Indian skin?

It can be performed across skin tones, but Indian skin may develop temporary or persistent pigmentation. Burns, infection and scarring are also possible when technique or hygiene is poor.

Can black and white hairs be treated in the same area?

Yes. Suitable dark hairs may be reduced with laser, while residual white hairs may require electrolysis or temporary mechanical removal.

Does shaving make white facial hair grow thicker?

No. Shaving cuts the hair at the skin surface and does not increase the number or biological thickness of hair follicles.

Consultation and laser hair reduction are available at both branches. Electrolysis is not separately confirmed in the current service matrix, so its availability, location and cost must be checked after assessment.
