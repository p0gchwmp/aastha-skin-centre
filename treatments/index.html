Medical Dermatology Treatments

What is medical dermatology?

Medical dermatology involves diagnosing and treating diseases affecting the skin, hair, scalp and nails.

Many skin conditions look alike. Itching may result from eczema, allergy, fungal infection, scabies, psoriasis or several other causes. Correct diagnosis is therefore more important than simply choosing a cream based on appearance.

Conditions commonly evaluated include:

Acne

Eczema

Atopic dermatitis

Contact dermatitis

Psoriasis

Rosacea

Urticaria and hives

Vitiligo

Lichen planus

Seborrhoeic dermatitis

Fungal infections

Bacterial skin infections

Viral warts

Molluscum contagiosum

Scabies

Nail disorders

Paediatric skin conditions

Sexually transmitted skin conditions

Suspicious moles and skin lesions

Treatment may involve:

Prescription medicines

Medicated cleansers and moisturisers

Medical-grade topical treatment

Oral medicines where indicated

Trigger identification

Lifestyle and skincare guidance

Selected laboratory investigations

Skin scraping or fungal testing

Patch testing in selected cases

Skin biopsy when clinically required

Long-term maintenance plans

Acne treatment is selected according to the type of acne, severity, affected areas, tendency to scar and previous treatment history.

The primary goals are to:

Control active breakouts

Reduce blocked pores

Prevent new scars

Maintenance treatment

Acne Scar Treatment

Which treatments are used for acne scars?

Acne scars are classified according to their structure and depth. Rolling scars, boxcar scars and ice-pick scars often require different approaches.

A personalised plan may combine two or more techniques because a single procedure rarely treats every scar pattern equally.

Acne-scar procedures may include:

MNRF

Microneedling

Subcision

TCA CROSS for selected ice-pick scars

Fractional CO₂ laser in suitable cases

PRP-supported procedures

Scar revision

Combination treatment plans

Factors considered before scar treatment

Scar type and depth

Active acne

Skin type

History of pigmentation

Previous procedures

Tendency to form keloids

Acceptable downtime

Treatment expectations

Complete removal of scars should not be promised. The aim is progressive improvement in texture and appearance.

MNRF Treatment

What is MNRF?

Microneedling radiofrequency, commonly called MNRF, delivers controlled radiofrequency energy through fine needles at selected depths in the skin.

It may be considered for concerns such as:

Atrophic acne scars

Uneven skin texture

Enlarged pores

Selected surgical or traumatic scars

Skin-remodelling concerns

The treatment depth, energy and number of sessions vary according to the concern and treatment area.

Temporary redness, swelling, tenderness or pinpoint crusting may occur after treatment. Aftercare and sun protection are important during recovery.

Suggested CTA: Learn About MNRF

Fractional CO₂ Laser

What is fractional CO₂ laser used for?

Fractional CO₂ laser creates controlled microscopic treatment zones in the skin to support resurfacing and collagen remodelling.

It may be considered for selected cases involving:

Acne scars

Chickenpox scars

Uneven texture

Certain surgical scars

Selected skin growths

Photo-ageing concerns

Not every patient is suitable for fractional CO₂ laser. Skin type, pigmentation tendency, active infection, scar pattern and ability to follow aftercare are assessed before treatment.

Patients may experience redness, swelling, crusting and temporary darkening during recovery.

Melasma, post-acne marks, freckles, sun spots, frictional pigmentation and deeper dermal pigmentation behave differently and may need different treatment plans.

Chemical Peels

What is a medical chemical peel?

A chemical peel applies a controlled solution to selected skin layers to produce exfoliation and support skin renewal.

Different peel formulations and strengths are used for different indications.

Chemical peels may be considered for:

Acne

Post-acne marks

Melasma

Uneven pigmentation

Dull-looking skin

Rough texture

Selected sun-damage concerns

The correct peel depends on the patient’s skin type, diagnosis, sensitivity, current medicines and previous procedures.

Possible short-term effects include redness, dryness, flaking or temporary sensitivity.

Laser Hair Reduction

How does laser hair reduction work?

Laser hair reduction uses controlled light energy to target pigment within actively growing hair follicles.

Because hairs grow in different cycles, a planned series of sessions is generally required. Maintenance sessions may also be needed over time.

Common treatment areas include:

Face

Upper lip

Chin

Neck

Underarms

Arms

Legs

Chest

Back

Bikini area

Full body

Treatment planning considers:

Skin type

Hair colour and thickness

Body area

Hormonal factors

Previous hair-removal methods

Medical history

Response to previous sessions

The correct term is laser hair reduction, not permanent hair removal. The clinic’s working record specifically advises against unsupported “permanent” claims.

Q-Switched Laser and Laser Toning

What is a Q-switched laser used for?

A Q-switched Nd:YAG laser delivers short pulses of energy and may be considered for selected pigment and tattoo concerns.

Possible indications include:

Certain superficial pigmentation

Selected dermal pigmentation

Laser toning

Tattoo removal

Carbon laser procedures

Suitability depends on the diagnosis, pigment depth, skin type and risk of post-treatment colour changes.

Not every dark patch should be treated with laser. Some pigmentation conditions may worsen if the diagnosis or laser parameters are inappropriate.

Suggested CTA: Explore Q-Switched Laser Treatment

Tattoo Removal

Can tattoos be removed with laser treatment?

Laser tattoo removal uses selected wavelengths to fragment tattoo pigment so that the body can gradually clear portions of it.

The response depends on:

Tattoo colour

Pigment depth

Ink composition

Tattoo age

Professional or amateur application

Skin type

Previous removal attempts

Body location

Multiple sessions may be required, and complete clearance cannot be guaranteed.

Temporary swelling, crusting, blistering or colour changes may occur. Strict aftercare is important.

Suggested CTA: Explore Tattoo Removal

IPL and Photofacial

What is IPL treatment?

Intense pulsed light, or IPL, uses a range of light wavelengths rather than a single laser wavelength.

It may be considered for selected concerns involving:

Uneven pigmentation

Sun-related skin changes

Redness

Photorejuvenation

Overall skin tone

A medical assessment is needed because IPL is not appropriate for every skin type or pigmentation concern.

PRP Hair Treatment

What is PRP hair therapy?

Platelet-rich plasma treatment uses a concentrated portion of the patient’s own blood containing platelets and associated growth factors.

It may be used as supportive treatment for selected patterns of hair thinning.

PRP is generally considered as part of a broader plan that may also include medicines, scalp care and treatment of contributing medical factors.

The number and frequency of sessions vary according to the diagnosis and response.

GFC Hair Treatment

What is GFC therapy for hair?

Growth Factor Concentrate, or GFC, is an autologous treatment prepared from the patient’s blood to obtain a concentrated growth-factor preparation.

It may be considered for selected types of hair thinning after medical evaluation.

Results vary according to:

Diagnosis

Duration of hair loss

Follicle condition

Age

Medical factors

Adherence to the overall treatment plan

Hair Transplantation

Who may be suitable for a hair transplant?

Hair transplantation moves suitable hair follicles from a donor area to an area affected by permanent pattern hair loss.

Suitability depends on:

Type of hair loss

Stability of hair loss

Donor-area density

Age and expected future progression

Scalp health

Medical history

Realistic expectations

A consultation should assess both current hair loss and the likely future pattern before surgery is planned.

Medical treatment may still be required after transplantation to protect existing non-transplanted hair.

Dandruff and Scalp Treatment

Does dandruff always mean dry scalp?

No. Dandruff may be associated with seborrhoeic dermatitis, scalp psoriasis, fungal overgrowth, product irritation or other inflammatory conditions.

Treatment may include:

Medicated shampoos

Anti-inflammatory scalp medicines

Antifungal treatment

Scale-control treatment

Hair-care modification

Maintenance therapy

Persistent itching, thick scaling, sores or hair loss should be medically assessed.

Botulinum Toxin Treatment

What is botulinum toxin used for?

Botulinum toxin temporarily reduces activity in selected muscles.

In aesthetic dermatology, it may be used to soften selected expression lines, including:

Forehead lines

Frown lines

Crow’s-feet

Other suitable dynamic lines

Treatment planning depends on facial anatomy, muscle movement, symmetry and the desired degree of correction.

The aim is controlled and natural-looking improvement rather than eliminating all facial movement.

Dermal Fillers

What are dermal fillers?

Dermal fillers are injectable materials used to restore or support selected areas of facial volume and contour.

They may be considered for:

Facial volume changes

Selected folds

Lip enhancement

Chin or jawline balance

Under-eye hollowing in suitable patients

Scar correction in selected cases

A detailed assessment is important because filler treatment affects facial anatomy and carries potential risks.

The amount, product type and treatment area should be individualised.

DPN and Seborrhoeic Keratosis Removal

What are DPN and seborrhoeic keratoses?

Dermatosis papulosa nigra, or DPN, causes small dark raised spots, commonly on the face and neck.

Seborrhoeic keratoses are benign growths that may appear waxy, rough or stuck onto the skin.

Treatment may be considered when the growths are irritated, cosmetically concerning or uncertain in diagnosis.

Possible methods include radiofrequency, cautery, curettage or other suitable procedures.

Nail Surgery and Ingrown Toenail Treatment

When is nail surgery required?

An ingrown toenail occurs when part of the nail edge presses into the surrounding skin.

Symptoms may include:

Pain

Swelling

Redness

Discharge

Recurrent infection

Overgrown tissue around the nail

Mild cases may improve with conservative treatment. Recurrent or severe cases may require partial nail surgery and treatment of the responsible nail-producing area.

Cyst and Lipoma Removal

Can sebaceous cysts and lipomas be removed?

Yes. Selected cysts and lipomas may be removed after clinical evaluation.

A cyst may become painful, inflamed or infected. An actively infected cyst may first need drainage or medical treatment before definitive removal.

A lipoma is usually a soft fatty lump beneath the skin. Examination helps determine whether the lump is clinically consistent with a lipoma or requires further investigation.

Skin Biopsy

Why is a skin biopsy performed?

A skin biopsy removes a small sample of tissue for laboratory examination.

It may be advised when:

The diagnosis is uncertain

A rash has unusual features

A lesion is changing

A skin growth requires confirmation

An inflammatory or autoimmune condition is suspected

Skin cancer needs to be ruled out

The biopsy method depends on the size, depth and location of the lesion.

Incision and Drainage

When is incision and drainage needed?

Incision and drainage may be performed for selected abscesses or infected collections containing pus.

The procedure relieves pressure and helps remove infected material.

Antibiotics may also be required depending on:

Size

Location

Surrounding infection

Fever

Medical history

Diabetes or immune status

Rapidly spreading redness, fever or severe pain may require urgent hospital assessment.

Cryolipolysis and Body Contouring

What is cryolipolysis?

Cryolipolysis is a non-surgical body-contouring treatment that cools selected areas of localised fat.

It is not a weight-loss treatment and is not a substitute for diet, exercise or medical management of obesity.

Suitability depends on:

Treatment area

Fat distribution

Skin and tissue condition

Medical history

Expectations

Possible temporary effects include redness, tenderness, numbness or swelling.

Paediatric Dermatology

What skin treatments are available for children?

Children may develop eczema, allergies, fungal infections, bacterial infections, viral warts, molluscum, scabies and other age-specific concerns.

Treatment is chosen according to:

The child’s age

Diagnosis

Body area

Severity

Weight where relevant

Medical history

Safety of the medicine for children

Parents should seek urgent care for fever with rash, breathing difficulty, extensive blistering, facial swelling or rapidly spreading redness.

How is a treatment plan prepared?

Step 1: Consultation

The concern, duration, symptoms, medical history, medicines and previous procedures are reviewed.

Step 2: Diagnosis

The skin, scalp, hair or nail concern is examined. Tests are advised only when clinically useful.

Step 3: Treatment options

Suitable medical, procedural and supportive options are explained.

Step 4: Expectations

The likely treatment course, limitations, downtime, aftercare and possible side effects are discussed.

Step 5: Consent and treatment

Procedures begin after patient understanding and consent.

Step 6: Follow-up

Response, side effects and the need for maintenance or additional sessions are reviewed.

Which treatments are available for acne scars?

Treatment may include MNRF, microneedling, subcision, TCA CROSS, fractional CO₂ laser or combination therapy. The choice depends on scar type, depth, skin type and active acne.
