Key point

Acne nodules are deep, firm and often painful inflammatory lesions. Unlike surface pimples, they develop deeper in the skin and carry a greater risk of permanent scarring.

How is an acne nodule different from a pimple?

A nodule is usually larger, firmer and deeper than a papule or pustule. It may not have a visible head and can remain tender for days or weeks.

Several deep lesions may be described as nodular acne and warrant timely medical assessment.

Why do nodules need early attention?

Deep inflammation can damage surrounding collagen and increase the risk of depressed or raised scars.

  • Pain and swelling can interfere with sleep or daily activity
  • Picking or attempted drainage can worsen tissue injury
  • New lesions may continue while older nodules are healing
  • Lingering dark or red marks are common after inflammation

How may nodular acne be treated?

A dermatologist assesses severity, distribution, scarring, previous medicines, medical history and pregnancy considerations. Deep acne often needs a prescription plan and monitoring rather than spot treatment alone.

Selected individual lesions may sometimes receive an in-clinic treatment, but the wider goal is to prevent new nodules.

When is urgent review needed?

Prompt review is appropriate for rapidly worsening pain, widespread deep acne, scarring, emotional distress or a lesion that may instead be an abscess. Fever or spreading redness needs urgent medical attention.

Frequently asked questions

Can a painful nodule be drained at home?

No. Home puncture or squeezing can worsen inflammation, infection and scarring. A clinician should confirm what the lesion is before any procedure.

Do acne nodules always scar?

Not every nodule causes a permanent scar, but the risk is higher than with superficial acne. Early control and avoiding picking may reduce risk.

Is a nodule the same as a cyst?

The terms are sometimes used together in everyday language, but dermatologists distinguish lesions by their clinical features. Both deep painful patterns deserve assessment.
