Key point

Acne papules are small, raised, inflamed bumps that usually look red, pink, brown or purple depending on skin tone. They do not have a visible pus-filled centre.

What causes acne papules?

A blocked follicle can become inflamed, producing a tender raised bump. Papules may develop alongside blackheads, whiteheads and pustules.

The colour of inflammation varies across skin tones, so acne severity should not be judged by redness alone.

Why should papules not be picked?

Picking can push inflammation deeper, delay healing and increase the chance of a dark mark or permanent scar.

  • Avoid needles, pinching and abrasive spot tools
  • Use gentle cleansing rather than repeated scrubbing
  • Keep hair and pore-blocking products away from acne-prone areas
  • Follow prescribed treatment consistently rather than only treating visible spots

Could a red bump be something other than acne?

Yes. Folliculitis, rosacea, perioral dermatitis, insect bites and product irritation can all produce red bumps. Itching, a sudden uniform eruption, unusual distribution or lack of comedones may suggest another diagnosis.

When should inflamed acne be treated medically?

Dermatology review is useful when papules are numerous, painful, recurrent, leaving marks or spreading to the chest and back. Early control may reduce the risk of progression and scarring.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a papule and a pustule?

A papule is an inflamed solid bump without visible pus. A pustule has a pale or yellow pus-filled centre.

Can papules turn into deeper acne?

Some inflamed lesions can become larger or deeper. Worsening tenderness, size or number is a reason to review the treatment plan.

Will every red pimple leave a mark?

No, but inflammation, picking and deeper skin tones can increase the likelihood of lingering colour change.
