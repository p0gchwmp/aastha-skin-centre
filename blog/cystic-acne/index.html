Key point

Cystic acne describes deep, painful inflammatory breakouts that may feel soft or fluctuant beneath the skin. Severe deep acne should be assessed early because delayed control can lead to lasting scars.

What are the signs of deep cystic acne?

Lesions are usually larger than ordinary pimples, sit deep in the skin and may remain painful for a prolonged period. They can occur on the face, jaw, neck, chest, shoulders or back.

Deep acne may occur with nodules, inflamed pustules, comedones and existing scars.

What increases the risk of scarring?

Depth and duration of inflammation matter. Repeated squeezing, delayed treatment and continuing new lesions add further risk.

  • Multiple deep lesions
  • A personal or family tendency to acne scars
  • Picking, puncturing or aggressive facials
  • Uncontrolled acne over several months
  • Breakouts on the chest, shoulders or back

How is cystic acne treatment planned?

The plan depends on severity, age, medical history, previous response and pregnancy potential. Prescription oral and topical treatment may be considered, with counselling and monitoring appropriate to the medicine selected.

Scar procedures are generally planned after active acne is controlled so that new damage is not continuing during scar treatment.

Could a deep lump be something else?

Yes. Boils, abscesses, inflamed cysts and hidradenitis suppurativa can resemble cystic acne. A hot, rapidly expanding, fluctuant or fever-associated lump requires prompt assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Can cystic acne be treated with facials alone?

No. Facials do not replace medical treatment for deep inflammatory acne and aggressive manipulation can worsen painful lesions.

When is isotretinoin considered?

A dermatologist may consider isotretinoin for selected severe, scarring or treatment-resistant acne. It requires careful supervision and strict pregnancy-prevention measures where relevant.

Should scar treatment start immediately?

Active acne is usually controlled first. Once new deep lesions are reduced, scar type and suitable procedures can be assessed separately.
