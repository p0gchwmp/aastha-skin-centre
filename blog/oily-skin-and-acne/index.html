Key point

Oily skin occurs when sebaceous glands produce more surface oil. It can accompany acne, but stripping the skin with harsh cleansers may increase irritation without solving blocked pores.

Does oily skin always mean acne?

No. Some people have oily skin without acne, while others develop acne because oil combines with follicular blockage and inflammation.

A shiny face alone is not a reason to use several strong active ingredients at once.

What is a sensible routine for oily acne-prone skin?

A simple routine is easier to tolerate and follow consistently.

  • Cleanse gently up to twice daily and after heavy sweating
  • Choose oil-free or non-comedogenic products
  • Use a lightweight moisturiser if treatment causes dryness
  • Apply suitable broad-spectrum sunscreen
  • Avoid alcohol-heavy astringents and abrasive scrubbing
  • Keep oily hair products away from acne-prone facial skin

Why can over-cleansing make things worse?

Frequent washing, hot water and scrubbing can disrupt the skin barrier, causing stinging, redness and poor tolerance of acne medicines. Irritated skin can look shinier and more inflamed.

Oil control should not come at the cost of barrier damage.

When should oily skin be assessed?

Seek advice when oiliness is accompanied by persistent comedones, inflamed acne, scarring, sudden adult-onset symptoms or irritation from multiple products. A clinician can simplify the routine and select treatment for the actual acne type.

Frequently asked questions

Should oily skin skip moisturiser?

Not always. Acne treatment can dry or irritate the skin, and a suitable non-comedogenic moisturiser can improve comfort and adherence.

How often should oily skin be washed?

Gentle cleansing up to twice daily and after sweating is usually sufficient. More frequent washing can irritate the skin.

Can sunscreen worsen oily skin?

A product that feels heavy may be uncomfortable, but sunscreen remains important. Look for a non-comedogenic formula suited to oily skin.
