Key point

An acne pustule is an inflamed pimple with a visible white or yellow centre. It forms when inflammation and immune cells collect within a blocked follicle.

What does an acne pustule look like?

A pustule is usually a small raised lesion with a pale centre and an inflamed base. It may feel tender and can occur with comedones or deeper acne.

Large, rapidly worsening or very painful pus-filled lesions may represent a boil, abscess or another infection rather than routine acne.

Why is popping a pustule risky?

Popping can spread inflammation into surrounding tissue and increases trauma to the follicle.

  • More swelling and tenderness
  • Post-inflammatory dark or red marks
  • Secondary infection
  • A higher chance of textural scarring

How are recurrent pustules assessed?

The distribution, presence of blackheads or whiteheads, use of steroids or cosmetics, shaving pattern and medication history help distinguish acne from folliculitis and other eruptions.

Treatment is selected according to the full acne pattern rather than the colour of one spot. Repeated antibiotics without an overall plan may not provide durable control.

Which warning signs need prompt care?

Seek prompt medical advice for spreading redness, fever, severe pain, rapidly enlarging swelling, lesions near the eye or a soft fluctuant lump that may be an abscess.

Frequently asked questions

Does pus always mean infection?

Not necessarily. Acne pustules contain inflammatory material within a follicle, but a boil or abscess is a different problem and may need urgent treatment.

Can acne pustules leave scars?

They can leave colour changes, while deeper or repeatedly traumatised lesions have a greater risk of permanent scarring.

Should an antibiotic cream be used on every pustule?

No. Unsupervised antibiotic use can be ineffective and contributes to resistance. Acne treatment should follow an appropriate diagnosis and complete plan.
