Key point

Blackheads are open comedones: small blocked pores whose contents are exposed at the skin surface. The dark colour is caused by oxidation, not trapped dirt, so harsh scrubbing is not the answer.

What exactly is a blackhead?

A blackhead develops when oil and dead skin cells collect inside a hair follicle. Because the pore remains open, the material at the surface reacts with air and looks brown or black.

Blackheads are common on the nose, forehead, chin, chest and back. They may occur alone or alongside whiteheads, inflamed pimples and oily skin.

What can make blackheads worse?

Blackheads are influenced by oil production, pore blockage and acne tendency. They are not proof that the skin is dirty.

  • Heavy or pore-blocking skin and hair products
  • Picking, squeezing or repeated metal-tool extraction
  • Aggressive scrubs that irritate the skin barrier
  • Stopping an effective acne routine too early
  • Hormonal changes that increase oil production

How are blackheads managed?

A dermatologist first confirms that the dark dots are comedones rather than normal sebaceous filaments, enlarged pores or another condition. A plan may include suitable topical acne treatment, gentle non-comedogenic skin care and medically supervised extraction for selected lesions.

Improvement is gradual. A maintenance plan is often needed because pores can block again even after successful extraction.

When should blackheads be examined?

Arrange an assessment if blackheads are widespread, repeatedly inflamed, leaving marks, occurring with painful acne or not improving despite consistent care. Sudden severe acne in adulthood or acne with hormonal symptoms may need a broader review.

Frequently asked questions

Can blackheads be permanently removed?

Individual blackheads can clear, but new ones can form because oil production and pore blockage may continue. Ongoing maintenance is usually more realistic than a permanent one-time removal.

Should I squeeze blackheads at home?

Squeezing can cause inflammation, infection, marks or scarring. Medically supervised extraction is safer for selected lesions and should be part of a wider acne plan.

Are nose dots always blackheads?

No. Sebaceous filaments are normal structures that can look like small grey dots, especially on the nose. Examination helps distinguish them from true open comedones.
