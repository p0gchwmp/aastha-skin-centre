When should you consult a dermatologist?

You should consider consulting a dermatologist when a skin, hair, nail or scalp concern:

Persists despite basic care

Keeps returning

Spreads to other body areas

Causes pain, discharge or bleeding

Disturbs sleep because of itching

Causes noticeable hair loss

Leaves marks or scars

Affects confidence or daily activities

Develops after starting a medicine

Has not been properly diagnosed

Seek urgent medical care when there is:

Difficulty breathing

Swelling of the face, tongue or throat

Fever with a widespread rash

Extensive blistering or skin peeling

Rapidly spreading redness

Severe pain or swelling

Eye involvement

A severe reaction after taking medicine

Emergency symptoms should be taken to an appropriate hospital or emergency department rather than managed through an online appointment request.

How is a skin or hair concern diagnosed?

Diagnosis usually begins with a medical history and physical examination. Depending on the concern, Dr. Cheena Langer may advise investigations such as:

Dermoscopy

Skin scraping

Fungal examination

Patch testing

Blood investigations

Hair or scalp assessment

Skin biopsy

Bacterial testing in selected cases

Not every patient requires tests. Investigations are recommended only when they can help confirm the diagnosis or guide treatment.

Which doctor should I consult for hair fall in Jammu?

A dermatologist can evaluate hair fall because hair loss may be connected to genetic, hormonal, nutritional, autoimmune or scalp-related conditions. Dr. Cheena Langer provides hair and scalp assessment at the Karan Nagar and Paloura clinics.

How can I tell whether a rash is fungal or an allergy?

A fungal infection and an allergic rash may both cause redness and itching, so appearance alone may not confirm the diagnosis. A dermatologist may use examination or selected tests before prescribing treatment.

When should acne be examined by a dermatologist?

Acne should be assessed when it is painful, persistent, leaving dark marks or scars, or not improving with basic care. Early treatment may help reduce the risk of permanent scarring.

Should a changing mole be examined?

Yes. A mole that changes in shape, colour or size, bleeds, becomes painful or looks different from the others should be assessed by a dermatologist.
